\begin{abstract}
  The two-parameter Gutman--Milovanovi\'c family
  $M_{\alpha,\beta}(G) = \sum_{uv \in E(G)} (d_u d_v)^{\alpha} (d_u + d_v)^{\beta}$
  unifies the Zagreb, Randi\'c, sum-connectivity and related degree-based
  indices. We study its normalized degree-imbalance exponential
  extension, the \emph{Loyola index family}
  \begin{equation*}
  LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
  \;=\; \sum_{uv \in E(G)}
  (d_u d_v)^{\alpha}\,(d_u + d_v)^{\beta}\,
  \exp\!\left(\gamma\,\frac{|d_u - d_v|}{d_u + d_v}\right),
  \end{equation*}
  with $LO(G;\alpha,\beta,0) = M_{\alpha,\beta}(G)$ identically.

  We characterise exactly what the imbalance coordinate can and cannot
  resolve. The family factorises into a degree-sum (scale) coordinate
  and a shape (degree-ratio) coordinate; on the resulting ratio plane the
  pure-$\gamma$ points separate exactly the histograms of edge
  imbalances (for algebraic $\gamma \neq 0$). Since these histograms
  coincide with degree-pair profiles on trees up to order $12$ (order
  $15$ for $\Delta \le 4$; enumeration), the pure-$\gamma$ points attain
  the discrimination limit of the whole vertex-degree-based class there,
  and provably lose information beyond it. We also bound the number of
  $\gamma$-crossings of two graphs, characterise per-graph
  identifiability, record routine termwise bounds for $\gamma \ge 0$,
  and note that $\log LO$ is
  convex (the standard log-sum-exp property). The predictive result is
  negative: an apparent gain for the vaporization enthalpy of the
  octanes and nonanes vanishes once the parent family is searched over a
  box that does not truncate it (a post hoc analysis), and pre-registered
  tests on measured
  boiling points (34 decanes; 100 alkanes C6--C10, adjusted for size)
  find no demonstrated benefit from $\gamma$ under these protocols,
  while ridge regression on the
  degree-pair counts predicts far better than any single index on these
  boiling-point sets. The
  untuned point $LO(0,0,1)$ is essentially the irregularity index
  $IRLA$. Hence $\gamma$ is presented as an interpretable,
  analytically tractable direction, not a predictive upgrade.
  \end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
  % =====================================================================

  The literature on degree-based topological indices in chemical
  graph theory has grown substantially since the seminal Wiener
  index~\cite{Wiener1947}, the Randi\'c index~\cite{Randic1975},
  and the Zagreb indices of Gutman and
  Trinajsti\'c~\cite{GutmanTrinajstic1972}.
  Among the many parametric extensions are the general Randi\'c
  index $R_{\alpha}$~\cite{BollobasErdos1998}, the general
  sum-connectivity index
  $\chi_{\alpha}$~\cite{ZhouTrinajstic2010}, the forgotten
  index $F$ revisited by Furtula and
  Gutman~\cite{FurtulaGutman2015F}, the geometric--arithmetic index
  $GA$~\cite{VukicevicFurtula2009GA}, the Albertson irregularity
  index~\cite{Albertson1997} and the inverse $\sigma$-index
  problem~\cite{GutmanToganYurttasCevikCangul2018Sigma}.

  A geometric strand was opened by the Sombor index
  family~\cite{Gutman2021Sombor, Gutman2021SomborBasic}, followed by work
  on its bounds~\cite{MilovanovicMilovanovicMatejic2021Sombor}, its
  relations to classical degree-based
  indices~\cite{WangMaoLiFurtula2022SomborRelations}, and its extremal
  values over molecular trees and related
  classes~\cite{DasCevikCangulShang2021Sombor, DasGutman2022SomborTrees,
  AhmadFarooqDas2025Extremal}. Its variants include the
  mean~\cite{MendezBermudezAguilarSanchez2022MeanSombor},
  elliptic~\cite{GutmanFurtulaOz2024Elliptic},
  hyperbolic~\cite{BarmanDas2026HSO},
  diminished~\cite{Movahedi2025DSO, MovahediGutmanRedzepovicFurtula2026}
  and KG-Sombor~\cite{KosariDehgardiKhan2023Lower} indices (see the
  survey~\cite{Gutman2026SomborSurvey}). Such descriptors are
  increasingly used in quantitative structure-property relationship
  (QSPR) and spectral studies~\cite{ArockiarajPaulClement2023Hybrid,
  KalaamGreeniArockiaraj2024}, and in matrix, coindex and simplicial
  formulations~\cite{ZuoRatherImranAli2022Modified,
  GujiWali2025Lanzhou, Shang2022Simplicial} and finite-dimensional
  linear frameworks~\cite{Rada2026LinearVDB}; extremal results on
  bond-incident-degree (BID) indices in general are surveyed
  in~\cite{AliGutmanFurtula2026BID}.


  The natural two-parameter unification of the product-based and
  sum-based degree indices is the \emph{Gutman--Milovanovi\'c family},
  introduced by Gutman, Milovanovi\'c and
  Milovanovi\'c~\cite{GutmanMilovanovic2020AKCE} and studied
  intensively in the recent bounds and QSPR literature
  \cite{GranadosJMC2025GM, MolinaSigarreta2025GM, PortillaJMC2025GM}:
  $M_{\alpha,\beta}(G) = \sum_{uv \in E(G)}
  (d_u d_v)^{\alpha} (d_u + d_v)^{\beta}$, $\alpha, \beta \in
  \mathbb{R}$. Its special cases (most of them catalogued
  in~\cite{MolinaSigarreta2025GM}) include the first and second Zagreb
  indices $M_{0,1} = M_1$ and $M_{1,0} = M_2$, the hyper-Zagreb index
  $M_{0,2} = HM$~\cite{ShirdelRezapourSayadi2013HM}, the modified second
  Zagreb index $M_{-1,0} = {}^{m}\!M_2$, the Randi\'c index
  $M_{-1/2,0} = R$, the sum-connectivity index
  $M_{0,-1/2} = \chi$~\cite{ZhouTrinajstic2009sumconn}, the harmonic
  index $2M_{0,-1} = H$, the inverse sum indeg index $M_{1,-1} = ISI$,
  the geometric-arithmetic index $2M_{1/2,-1} = GA$, the
  arithmetic-geometric index $\tfrac12 M_{-1/2,1} = AG$, and, as
  parameter axes, the general Randi\'c family
  $R_\alpha = M_{\alpha,0}$~\cite{BollobasErdos1998} and general
  sum-connectivity family
  $\chi_\beta = M_{0,\beta}$~\cite{ZhouTrinajstic2010}.
  Parameter-optimized versions of this family and of related
  generalized descriptors have already been fitted to octane
  properties~\cite{AliGutmanFurtulaAlbalahiHamza2025}. We claim no
  novelty for this backbone, for its reduction catalogue, or for its
  generic tunability: those belong to the Gutman--Milovanovi\'c
  literature.

  In this paper we study a three-parameter extension of
  $M_{\alpha,\beta}$, the \emph{Loyola index family}, obtained by
  attaching a separately parameterized exponential factor in the
  normalized degree imbalance $q_{uv} = |d_u - d_v|/(d_u + d_v)$ of each
  edge: $LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma) = \sum_{uv}
  (d_u d_v)^{\alpha}(d_u+d_v)^{\beta} e^{\gamma q_{uv}}$, so that
  $LO(G;\alpha,\beta,0) = M_{\alpha,\beta}(G)$ identically. Positive
  $\gamma$ exponentially upweights degree-imbalanced edges, negative
  $\gamma$ downweights them, and on regular graphs $\gamma$ has no
  effect. The questions this paper answers are therefore about $\gamma$
  alone: is the imbalance direction mathematically independent of the
  parent parameters, what analytic structure does it carry, and does it
  add measurable structural or predictive information beyond an
  optimized Gutman--Milovanovi\'c parent?

  Writing the summand as
  $\exp\bigl(\alpha \ln(d_u d_v) + \beta \ln(d_u + d_v) +
  \gamma\, q_{uv}\bigr)$ shows that the family is a
  three-parameter subfamily of the exponential vertex-degree-based
  indices introduced by Rada~\cite{Rada2019ExpVDB} and studied in
  extremal settings by Sigarreta~\cite{Sigarreta2022ExpVDB}; general
  sufficient conditions for tree-extremal behaviour of such exponential
  edge-weight sums are known~\cite{GaoAMC2024} (see
  also~\cite{CruzMonsalveRada2020, DasElumalaiBalachandran2021}), bounds
  for general edge-weight functions over graphs of given order and size
  are given in~\cite{HuMATCH2022}, and no extremal-ordering novelty is
  claimed here. The published one-parameter exponential indices (e.g.\
  the exponential geometric--arithmetic~\cite{DasMondal2024ExpGA} and
  exponential inverse symmetric division
  deg~\cite{DasMondalRazaPal2025ExpISDD} indices) place their parameter
  on a single classical generator inside the exponent; the scoped
  contribution of this family is the multi-parameter \emph{linear
  combination} in the exponent in which the two Gutman--Milovanovi\'c
  basis terms act as the factors $(d_u d_v)^{\alpha}(d_u + d_v)^{\beta}$
  while the imbalance coordinate carries its own parameter $\gamma$. The
  imbalance ratio itself appears in many published indices: the
  diminished Sombor index, with edge weight
  $\sqrt{(1+q_{uv}^2)/2}$~\cite{MovahediGutmanRedzepovicFurtula2026};
  the irregularity index $IRLA(G) = 2\sum_{uv}
  q_{uv}$~\cite{RetiSharafdini2018IRLA}, of which the pure-$\gamma$ point
  $LO(G;0,0,\gamma) = \sum_{uv} e^{\gamma q_{uv}}$ is the exponential
  index in the sense of~\cite{Rada2019ExpVDB}; degree-ratio
  indices~\cite{NyauliBuragohain2027DRSO, GutmanBuragohainNyauli2026};
  and the parent family itself, since
  $1 - q_{uv}^2 = 4 d_u d_v/(d_u + d_v)^2$ gives
  $M_{\alpha,-2\alpha}(G) = 4^{-\alpha} \sum_{uv} (1 - q_{uv}^2)^{\alpha}$
  (the variable geometric--arithmetic direction). The parent family is
  therefore not blind to imbalance; what is new is a coordinate
  \emph{linear} in $q_{uv}$ in the exponent, which, unlike
  $\ln(1 - q_{uv}^2)$, is not an affine function of $\ln(d_u d_v)$ and
  $\ln(d_u + d_v)$ (Lemma~\ref{prop:indep}). To our knowledge, and
  within the scope of the literature search documented in the
  reproducibility package, no published index couples it
  multiplicatively, with its own parameter, to the Gutman--Milovanovi\'c
  family. Since $0 \le q_{uv} < 1$, the factor lies in $[1, e^{\gamma})$
  for $\gamma > 0$, equals $1$ for $\gamma = 0$, lies in
  $(e^{\gamma}, 1]$ for $\gamma < 0$, and reduces to $1$ on regular
  graphs. The exponential form is not derived from a physical model; it
  is chosen because it makes the family log-linear in its parameters, so
  that $\gamma$ acts multiplicatively and only on irregularity, and the
  whole family is an exponential family in $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$,
  which is what makes the convexity, identifiability and discrimination
  results of Section~\ref{sec:geometry} available.


  On hydrogen-suppressed molecular graphs with $\Delta \le 4$, every BID
  index is a linear functional of the ten edge-degree-pair counts
  $m_{ij}(G) = |\{uv \in E(G) : \{d_u, d_v\} = \{i, j\}\}|$,
  $1 \le i \le j \le 4$ (cf.\ the linear framework of
  Rada~\cite{Rada2026LinearVDB}), so the Loyola family lies inside a fixed
  ten-dimensional descriptor space, however its parameters are chosen.


  \emph{Scope of the contribution.} Four questions should be answered
  at the outset. First, is the family sufficiently novel, given $IRLA$
  and the existing exponential vertex-degree-based indices? The edge
  invariant $q_{uv}$ is classical, and the pure-$\gamma$ point
  $LO(0,0,\gamma)$ is simply the exponential index, in the sense
  of~\cite{Rada2019ExpVDB}, of the $IRLA$ edge weights. What is new is
  not a new edge invariant but its embedding as a free, log-linear
  coordinate inside the Gutman--Milovanovi\'c family, and the exact
  description of the resulting three-parameter structure: a scale--shape
  factorisation, a ratio plane on which discrimination is characterised
  exactly, and per-graph identifiability. Second, several of the
  mathematical results are applications of standard tools: the
  convexity of log-sum-exp functions, Laguerre's rule of signs, the
  Lindemann--Weierstrass theorem and the minimality theory of
  exponential families. We claim no novelty for these tools; the
  contribution is their application to this family and the structural
  characterisation that results (the exact $q$-histogram ceiling of the
  pure-$\gamma$ line, colliding tree families at every order from $13$,
  a bound on $\gamma$-crossings, and the classification of
  scale-invariant directions). Third, why use a three-parameter family
  if $IRLA$ performs similarly? Not for predictive superiority, which
  we do not find. The reasons are interpretability within the parent
  family (every member is a deformation of a classical
  Gutman--Milovanovi\'c index, recovered at $\gamma = 0$); continuous
  structural deformation (size and shape are controlled separately, and
  $\gamma$ moves continuously from the parent towards imbalance-weighted
  indices); ratio-plane geometry (the pure-$\gamma$ line, $GA$ and $AG$
  lie in one plane on which their discriminating power is described
  exactly); and analytic tractability (log-linearity gives convexity,
  closed-form derivatives such as
  $\partial_{\gamma}\log LO = \mathbb{E}_{\theta}[q]$, and an exact
  identifiability theory). $IRLA$ is, to first order in $\gamma$, a
  single slice of this structure. Fourth, the QSPR datasets are small:
  $18$ octanes, $35$ nonanes, $34$ decanes with usable measured boiling
  points, and $100$ alkanes C6--C10 in a pooled size-adjusted set. We
  therefore present the chemometric
  component as diagnostic evidence about what imbalance information
  does and does not add to the parent family, not as proof of chemical
  predictive utility.

  In sum, the contributions are: (i) the scale--shape factorisation and
  the ratio-plane theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:ratioplane}), which
  characterises exactly what the pure-$\gamma$ line can discriminate and
  is in large part a limitation result (tree collisions at every order
  from $13$, from $16$ for $\Delta \le 4$); (ii) the crossing bound
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:laguerre}) and per-graph identifiability
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:identrank}), resting on an elementary
  independence lemma (Lemma~\ref{prop:indep}); (iii) log-convexity with
  mean/covariance derivatives (Theorem~\ref{thm:logconvex}, standard),
  closed forms and routine termwise bounds for $\gamma \ge 0$; (iv) the
  degree-pair ceiling, an elementary observation that is realised on the
  octane benchmark (Proposition~\ref{prop:collision}), and structure
  sensitivity; and
  (v) a negative, reproducible QSPR diagnostic: freeing $\gamma$ gives no
  consistent gain once the parent's search box does not truncate it,
  complemented by pre-registered tests on measured boiling points of
  decanes and of pooled C6--C10 alkanes (Section~\ref{sec:bp}), where
  ridge regression on the degree-pair counts is far ahead of every
  single index. The family is offered as an analytically transparent,
  interpretable third direction on the Gutman--Milovanovi\'c parameter
  space, not as a better predictor.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Notation}\label{sec:notation}

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a finite simple connected graph of order
$n = |V|$ and size $m = |E|$. For a vertex $v \in V$, the degree
$d_v = d_G(v)$ counts the edges incident to $v$. The maximum and
minimum degrees are denoted $\Delta = \Delta(G)$ and
$\delta = \delta(G)$. In the chemometric sections we work with
hydrogen-suppressed molecular graphs, where the assumption
$\Delta \le 4$ holds for alkane carbon skeletons (all graphs analysed
here) and for most organic molecules, but not universally: hypervalent
centres such as phosphorus in $\mathrm{PCl}_5$ (degree $5$) or sulfur in
pentafluorosulfanyl groups (degree $6$) exceed it.

The classical graph families used are the complete graph $K_n$, the
cycle $C_n$, the path $P_n$, the star $S_n$, the complete bipartite
graph $K_{p,q}$, the hypercube $Q_k$ ($2^k$ vertices,
$k \cdot 2^{k - 1}$ edges, $k$-regular), the wheel $W_n$ (an
$(n - 1)$-cycle plus a universal vertex), the friendship graph $F_p$
($p$ triangles sharing a common vertex), and the $r$-regular graphs.
For an edge $e = uv \in E(G)$ we write $(d_u, d_v)$ for its
\emph{degree pair}, understood as an unordered multiset
$\{d_u, d_v\}$.


\subsection{Definition}\label{sec:def}

\begin{definition}[Loyola index family]\label{def:wuzi}
Let $G = (V, E)$ be a simple connected graph. For
$\alpha, \beta, \gamma \in \mathbb{R}$, the \emph{Loyola index family}
is defined as,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wuzi}
\Wuzi
\;=\; \sum_{uv \in E(G)}
(d_u d_v)^{\alpha}\,(d_u + d_v)^{\beta}\,
\exp\!\left(\gamma\,\frac{|d_u - d_v|}{d_u + d_v}\right)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Equation~\eqref{eq:wuzi} is well defined for all
$\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{R}$ on graphs without isolated vertices
(so that $d_u, d_v \ge 1$ on every edge). The expression is
invariant under permutations of $(u, v)$ because
$d_u d_v = d_v d_u$, $d_u + d_v = d_v + d_u$, and
$|d_u - d_v| = |d_v - d_u|$; hence $\Wuzi$ depends only on the
multiset of unordered edge degree pairs of~$G$. When $\gamma = 0$
the family reduces identically to the Gutman--Milovanovi\'c
family~\cite{GutmanMilovanovic2020AKCE, MolinaSigarreta2025GM},
\begin{equation*}
LO(G;\alpha,\beta,0) \;=\; M_{\alpha,\beta}(G)
\;=\; \sum_{uv \in E(G)} (d_u d_v)^{\alpha} (d_u + d_v)^{\beta},
\end{equation*}
whose classical special cases are catalogued in the Introduction.
Throughout, $q_{uv} = \mathrm{imb}(d_u, d_v)$, where
$\mathrm{imb}(x, y) = |x - y|/(x + y)$, denotes the
normalized degree imbalance of the edge $uv$; positive $\gamma$
exponentially upweights degree-imbalanced edges, negative $\gamma$
downweights them, and on regular graphs every $q_{uv} = 0$ so that
$\gamma$ has no effect and
$LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma) = M_{\alpha,\beta}(G)$ for all $\gamma$.
\subsection{Closed-form values on standard graphs}\label{sec:closedforms}

  \begin{proposition}\label{prop:closedforms}
  Let $\alpha, \beta, \gamma \in \mathbb{R}$. Then:
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item  $LO(K_n; \alpha, \beta, \gamma) =
    \dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}\,2^{\beta}(n - 1)^{2\alpha + \beta}$ for $n \ge 2$.
  \item  $LO(C_n; \alpha, \beta, \gamma) = n \cdot 4^{\alpha +
  \beta}$
    for $n \ge 3$.
  \item
    $LO(P_n; \alpha, \beta, \gamma) =
    \begin{cases}
      2^{\beta}, & n = 2,\\[2pt]
      2 \cdot 2^{\alpha} 3^{\beta} e^{\gamma/3}, & n = 3,\\[2pt]
      2 \cdot 2^{\alpha} 3^{\beta} e^{\gamma/3} + (n - 3) \cdot 4^{\alpha +
  \beta},
         & n \ge 4.
    \end{cases}$
  \item  $LO(S_n; \alpha, \beta, \gamma) =
    (n - 1)\,(n - 1)^{\alpha} n^{\beta} \exp\!\left(\gamma\,\dfrac{n -
  2}{n}\right)$
    for $n \ge 2$.
  \item  $LO(K_{p,q}; \alpha, \beta, \gamma) =
    pq\,(pq)^{\alpha}(p + q)^{\beta}\exp\!\left(\gamma\,\dfrac{|p - q|}{p +
  q}\right)$.
  \item  $LO(Q_k; \alpha, \beta, \gamma) =
    k \cdot 2^{k - 1} \cdot 2^{\beta} k^{2\alpha + \beta}$ for $k \ge 1$.
  \item  For $n \ge 5$, $W_n$ has $n - 1$ spoke edges with
    degree pair $(3, n - 1)$ and $n - 1$ rim edges with degree pair
    $(3, 3)$, so
    \[
    LO(W_n; \alpha, \beta, \gamma)
    = (n - 1)\Bigl[\,3^{\alpha} (n - 1)^{\alpha}(n + 2)^{\beta}
         \exp\!\left(\gamma\,\tfrac{n - 4}{n + 2}\right)
       + 9^{\alpha} 6^{\beta}\Bigr].
    \]
  \item  For $p \ge 1$, $F_p$ has $2p$ spoke edges
    $(2, 2p)$ and $p$ outer edges $(2, 2)$, so
    \[
    LO(F_p; \alpha, \beta, \gamma)
    = p \cdot 4^{\alpha + \beta}
     + 2p\,(4p)^{\alpha}(2p + 2)^{\beta}
         \exp\!\left(\gamma\,\tfrac{p - 1}{p + 1}\right).
    \]
  \item  For an $r$-regular graph $G$ with $m$ edges,
    $\Wuzi = m \cdot 2^{\beta} r^{2\alpha + \beta}$.
  \end{enumerate}
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
  Each case follows by enumerating the edge degree pairs and applying
  Definition~\ref{def:wuzi}. In $K_n$, $C_n$, $Q_k$ and $r$-regular
  graphs every edge has $d_u = d_v = r$, so $\mathrm{imb} = 0$ and
  $(d_u d_v)^{\alpha}(d_u + d_v)^{\beta} = 2^{\beta} r^{2\alpha + \beta}$,
  which gives items (1), (2), (6) and (9). All $n - 1$ edges of $S_n$
  have degree pair $(1, n - 1)$ with $\mathrm{imb} = (n - 2)/n$
  (item (4)); all $pq$ edges of $K_{p,q}$ have pair $(p, q)$ (item (5));
  $P_n$, $n \ge 4$, has two pendant edges $(1, 2)$ and $n - 3$ interior
  edges $(2, 2)$, and the cases $n = 2, 3$ are immediate (item (3));
  items (7) and (8) follow by summing over the two edge-degree-pair
  classes of $W_n$ and $F_p$.
  \end{proof}

  \section{Parameter geometry and the role of the imbalance
  parameter}\label{sec:geometry}

  This section collects the structural results. Throughout,
  $z_{uv} = \bigl(\ln(d_u d_v),\, \ln(d_u + d_v),\, q_{uv}\bigr)$ is
  the feature vector of the edge $uv$ and
  $\theta = (\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$, so that
  $LO_G(\theta) = \sum_{uv \in E(G)} e^{\theta^{\top} z_{uv}}$. The
  independence result below is a short computation (almost any edge
  feature that is not log-linear in the degrees would pass the same
  test).


  \begin{lemma}[Affine independence of the imbalance
  direction]\label{prop:indep}
  Let $\Delta \ge 3$ and let
  $P_{\Delta} = \{(i,j) : 1 \le i \le j \le \Delta\}$ be the
  admissible degree pairs. Then the augmented feature vectors
  $\bigl(1, \ln(ij), \ln(i+j), q_{ij}\bigr)$, $(i,j) \in P_{\Delta}$,
  span $\mathbb{R}^4$. Consequently, if two parameter triples
  $\theta, \theta'$ satisfy
  $e^{\theta^{\top} z} = c\, e^{\theta'^{\top} z}$ for some constant
  $c > 0$ and all $(i,j) \in P_{\Delta}$, then $\theta = \theta'$ and
  $c = 1$; in particular no choice of $(\alpha', \beta')$ and scaling
  constant reproduces the action of $\gamma \neq 0$ on all admissible
  degree pairs. For $\Delta = 2$ the span has dimension $3$, and for
  $\Delta = 1$ (the single pair $(1,1)$, augmented vector
  $(1, 0, \ln 2, 0)$) it has dimension $1$; in both cases the
  conclusion fails.
  \end{lemma}

  \begin{proof}
  It suffices to show that $q_{ij}$ is not an affine function of
  $\bigl(\ln(ij), \ln(i+j)\bigr)$ on $P_{\Delta}$; the four pairs
  $(1,1), (2,2), (1,2), (1,3)$, all admissible once $\Delta \ge 3$,
  witness this. Suppose
  $q_{ij} = a + b \ln(ij) + c \ln(i+j)$ on these pairs. From
  $(1,1)$ and $(2,2)$, where $q = 0$: $a + c \ln 2 = 0$ and
  $a + (2b + 2c) \ln 2 = 0$, giving $a = -c \ln 2$ and $b = -c/2$.
  Substituting into the $(1,2)$ and $(1,3)$ equations gives
  $\tfrac13 = c\,(\ln 3 - \tfrac32 \ln 2)$ and
  $\tfrac12 = c\,(\ln 2 - \tfrac12 \ln 3)$; in particular $c \neq 0$.
  Three times the first minus twice the second gives
  $0 = c\,(4 \ln 3 - \tfrac{13}{2} \ln 2)$, hence $8 \ln 3 = 13 \ln 2$,
  i.e.\ $3^{8} = 2^{13}$, which is false ($6561 \neq 8192$). Hence the
  augmented vectors have rank $4$. If
  $e^{\theta^{\top} z} = c\, e^{\theta'^{\top} z}$ on $P_{\Delta}$,
  then $(\theta - \theta')^{\top} z_{ij} = \ln c$ for all admissible
  pairs, i.e.\ $(\ln c, \theta' - \theta)$ is orthogonal to every
  augmented vector, so $\theta = \theta'$ and $c = 1$. For
  $\Delta = 2$ only the pairs $(1,1), (1,2), (2,2)$ exist and a
  direct computation shows the augmented vectors have rank $3$; for
  $\Delta = 1$ the single augmented vector has rank $1$.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[Independence versus per-graph
  identifiability]\label{rem:ident}
  Lemma~\ref{prop:indep} concerns the edge-weight
  \emph{function} on the full admissible pair set, not every individual
  graph: on a single graph $G$ some direction $v$ satisfies
  $LO(G;\theta + tv) = e^{ct}\,LO(G;\theta)$ for all $t$ unless the
  realised feature vectors $z_{ij}$ affinely span $\mathbb{R}^3$
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:identrank}); along such a direction the index
  only rescales. On a regular graph nothing about $\theta$
  is identifiable beyond one linear functional; if the edges of $G$
  realise at most three affinely independent augmented vectors, some
  direction of $\theta$ --- possibly the $\gamma$ direction --- is
  unidentifiable from $LO(G;\cdot)$ alone. The lemma guarantees
  that no such degeneracy is forced on the whole molecular class
  $\Delta \ge 3$: the family separates parameters as a family of
  descriptors, even though particular graphs may not.
  \end{remark}


  The next result is the standard convexity of a log-sum-exp
  (log-partition) function, whose gradient and Hessian are the mean and
  covariance of the exponent's feature vector under the induced
  weights; we state it for $LO$ because these moments have a direct
  interpretation here and because strictness depends on the degree
  pairs that $G$ realises.

  \begin{theorem}[Global log-convexity]\label{thm:logconvex}
  Fix a graph $G$ with $m \ge 1$ edges and define
  $F_G(\theta) = \log LO_G(\theta)$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$. Let
  $\mathbb{E}_{\theta}$ and $\operatorname{Cov}_{\theta}$ denote mean
  and covariance under the edge probability measure
  $w_{uv}(\theta) = e^{\theta^{\top} z_{uv}} / LO_G(\theta)$. Then
  $F_G$ is convex, with
  \begin{equation*}
  \nabla F_G(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\theta}[z], \qquad
  \nabla^2 F_G(\theta) = \operatorname{Cov}_{\theta}(z) \succeq 0 .
  \end{equation*}
  In particular
  $\partial_{\gamma} F_G = \mathbb{E}_{\theta}[q] \in [0, 1)$ and
  $\partial^2_{\gamma} F_G = \operatorname{Var}_{\theta}(q) \ge 0$,
  so $\log LO$ is nondecreasing and convex in $\gamma$, strictly
  increasing in $\gamma$ unless every edge of $G$ joins vertices of
  equal degree. Moreover
  $\operatorname{Var}_{\theta}(q) = 0$ at some (equivalently, every)
  $\theta$ if and only if $q_{uv}$ is constant over the edges of $G$:
  this covers regular graphs ($q \equiv 0$), but also, e.g.,
  $(\Delta,\delta)$-biregular graphs, on which every edge carries the
  same nonzero imbalance and $\log LO$ is affine and strictly
  increasing in $\gamma$. $F_G$ is strictly convex if and only if the
  edge feature vectors $\{z_{uv}\}_{uv \in E}$ do not lie in a proper
  affine subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$; on a regular graph
  $F_G(\theta) = \log m + \theta^{\top} z_0$ is affine and
  $\operatorname{Cov}_{\theta}(z) = 0$.
  \end{theorem}

  \begin{proof}
  Direct differentiation of
  $LO_G(\theta) = \sum_{uv} e^{\theta^{\top} z_{uv}}$ gives
  $\partial_k F_G = \sum_{uv} w_{uv} z_{uv,k} =
  \mathbb{E}_{\theta}[z_k]$ and
  $\partial_k \partial_l F_G =
  \mathbb{E}_{\theta}[z_k z_l] -
  \mathbb{E}_{\theta}[z_k]\,\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[z_l]$, the
  $(k,l)$-entry of $\operatorname{Cov}_{\theta}(z)$, which is positive
  semidefinite; convexity follows. The measure $w(\theta)$ has full
  support on $E$ for every $\theta$, so
  $\operatorname{Cov}_{\theta}(z)$ is singular in a direction $v$ iff
  $v^{\top} z_{uv}$ is constant over all edges, i.e.\ iff the
  $z_{uv}$ lie in a proper affine subspace; this proves the strictness
  criterion. Since $q_{uv} \in [0,1)$,
  $\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[q] \in [0,1)$, and
  $\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[q] = 0$ iff $q_{uv} = 0$ on every edge. On an
  $r$-regular graph all $z_{uv}$ equal
  $z_0 = (2\ln r, \ln 2r, 0)$.
  \end{proof}

  Theorem~\ref{thm:logconvex} places the family in the classical
  exponential-family framework: moving $\gamma$ reweights the edge
  measure toward imbalanced edges at rate
  $\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[q]$, and the Cauchy--Schwarz midpoint
  inequality of Section~\ref{sec:bounds} is precisely midpoint
  log-convexity, i.e.\ a corollary of this theorem.

  \subsection{Size--shape factorisation, the ratio plane, crossings
  and identifiability}\label{sec:ratioplane}

  Since $4ij/(i+j)^2 = 1 - q_{ij}^2$, every admissible pair satisfies
  $\ln(ij) = 2\ln(i+j) + \ln(1 - q_{ij}^2) - \ln 4$, and therefore
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:factor}
  LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma) = 4^{-\alpha} \sum_{uv \in E(G)}
  (d_u + d_v)^{2\alpha+\beta}\,(1 - q_{uv}^2)^{\alpha}\, e^{\gamma q_{uv}}.
  \end{equation}
  The combination $\kappa = 2\alpha + \beta$ acts only on the edge
  degree sum (scale), while $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ act only on $q_{uv}$,
  which is a function of the degree ratio alone (shape). Write
  $n_q(G)$ for the number of edges of $G$ with $q_{uv} = q$ (the
  $q$-histogram), and $\Pi = \{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma) : \beta = -2\alpha\}$
  for the plane $\kappa = 0$, which contains every pure-$\gamma$ point
  $(0,0,\gamma)$ as well as $GA = 2\,LO(\tfrac12,-1,0)$ and
  $AG = \tfrac12 LO(-\tfrac12,1,0)$.

  \begin{theorem}[Ratio plane]\label{thm:ratioplane}
  Let $G$ and $H$ be graphs.
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] On $\Pi$,
  $LO(G;\alpha,-2\alpha,\gamma) = \sum_q n_q(G)\,\bigl((1-q^2)/4\bigr)^{\alpha}
  e^{\gamma q}$ depends on $G$ only through its $q$-histogram. $G$ and
  $H$ agree at every point of $\Pi$ if and only if $n_q(G) = n_q(H)$
  for all $q$; otherwise their values differ at every point of $\Pi$
  outside a set of measure zero.
  \item[(ii)] For every nonzero algebraic $\gamma$,
  $LO(G;0,0,\gamma) = LO(H;0,0,\gamma)$ if and only if
  $n_q(G) = n_q(H)$ for all $q$. On any finite set of graphs the same
  holds for all but finitely many real $\gamma$.
  \item[(iii)] Let $G$ and $H$ be trees with distinct
  edge-degree-pair profiles and equal $q$-histograms, each having a
  leaf adjacent to a vertex of the same degree $d \ge 2$. Attaching a
  pendant path of length $L \ge 1$ at such a leaf in each produces
  trees with the same two properties and the same maximum degree.
  Consequently, for every $n \ge 13$ (and every $n \ge 16$ among trees
  with $\Delta \le 4$) there are non-isomorphic trees of order $n$
  with distinct degree-pair profiles that no point of $\Pi$ separates.
  \end{enumerate}
  \end{theorem}

  \begin{proof}
  (i) is immediate from~\eqref{eq:factor}: setting $\beta = -2\alpha$
  removes the degree-sum factor; the substance of the theorem lies in
  (ii) and (iii). Agreement for equal histograms is immediate. If the
  histograms differ, at $\alpha = 0$ the difference is
  $\sum_q (n_q(G) - n_q(H))\,e^{\gamma q}$, an exponential sum with
  distinct exponents and not all coefficients zero, which is not
  identically zero; the difference on $\Pi$ is therefore a nonzero
  real-analytic function of $(\alpha,\gamma)$, whose zero set has
  measure zero. (ii) For algebraic $\gamma \neq 0$ the exponents
  $\gamma q$, $q \in \mathbb{Q}$, are distinct algebraic numbers, so
  by the Lindemann--Weierstrass theorem~\cite{Baker1975} the numbers
  $e^{\gamma q}$ are linearly independent over the algebraic numbers;
  the integer coefficients $n_q(G) - n_q(H)$ must all vanish. For real
  $\gamma$, a nonzero exponential sum with $k$ distinct real exponents
  has at most $k - 1$ real zeros (Proposition~\ref{prop:laguerre}),
  and a finite set of graphs gives finitely many such sums. (iii) The
  attachment changes the profile of each tree by the same vector,
  $-(1,d) + (2,d) + (L-1)(2,2) + (1,2)$, so distinct profiles stay
  distinct, equal histograms stay equal, and the new or changed
  vertices have degree at most $2$, so the maximum degree is unchanged
  when it is at least $2$. The order-$13$ pair of
  Section~\ref{sec:degeneracy} has a common leaf-neighbour degree
  $d = 3$, and the order-$16$ pair with $\Delta \le 4$,
  $\{(1,2)^{2}, (1,4)^{8}, (2,4)^{2}, (4,4)^{3}\}$ and
  $\{(1,4)^{8}, (2,2)^{3}, (2,4)^{4}\}$, has $d = 4$; this gives every
  larger order.
  \end{proof}

  Theorem~\ref{thm:ratioplane} is as much a limitation result as a
  characterisation. By exhaustive enumeration, $q$-histograms coincide with degree-pair
  profiles for all trees of order at most $12$ and for trees with
  $\Delta \le 4$ of order at most $15$, so the pure-$\gamma$ points
  attain the degree-pair floor exactly up to those orders and,
  by part~(iii), at no larger order. Theorem~\ref{thm:ratioplane} is
  consistent with the tie of $GA$, $AG$ and the pure-$\gamma$ points on
  the trees of order $10$ (Section~\ref{sec:degeneracy}): all lie on
  $\Pi$, so none can separate more than the $q$-histograms (that they
  separate all of them there is a computational fact). It also shows the
  limits of the pure-$\gamma$ line: at orders $13$--$17$, the orders
  enumerated, generic points off
  $\Pi$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:generic}) and the Randi\'c index (which
  itself leaves the floor only at order $14$) separate more trees than
  any point of $\Pi$. In the other
  direction, $GA$, whose values are sums of square roots, merges two
  distinct $q$-histograms among trees first at order $17$ (exact
  witness: $\{(1,5)^{10}, (2,2)^{2}, (5,5)^{2}, (2,5), (1,2)\}$ and
  $\{(4,5)^{3}, (1,4)^{5}, (1,5)^{6}, (2,5), (1,2)\}$ have equal $GA$
  but different $q$-histograms), where the pure-$\gamma$ points,
  by part~(ii), still separate all histograms. That $GA$ depends only
  on the degree ratio is well known; the content here is the exact
  ceiling and the colliding families.

  Along the $\gamma$ axis the number of times two graphs can exchange
  order is bounded by a classical rule of signs.

  \begin{proposition}[$\gamma$-crossings]\label{prop:laguerre}
  Fix $(\alpha,\beta)$ and graphs $G$, $H$, and let
  $q_1 < \dots < q_r$ be the distinct imbalances of the degree pairs
  occurring in $G$ or $H$. Write
  $D(\gamma) = LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma) - LO(H;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
  = \sum_{k=1}^{r} c_k e^{\gamma q_k}$ with
  $c_k = \sum_{q_{ij} = q_k} (m_{ij}(G) - m_{ij}(H))(ij)^{\alpha}(i+j)^{\beta}$,
  and let $V$ be the number of sign changes in $(c_1,\dots,c_r)$ after
  zero terms are removed. If $D \not\equiv 0$, then $D$ has at most
  $V \le r - 1$ real zeros counted with multiplicity, and their number
  has the parity of $V$. In particular, for graphs with $\Delta \le 4$,
  where the possible imbalances are $0, \tfrac17, \tfrac15, \tfrac13,
  \tfrac12, \tfrac35$, two graphs exchange order at most five times as
  $\gamma$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$, for every $(\alpha,\beta)$.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
  With $x = e^{\gamma} > 0$, $D$ becomes the generalised polynomial
  $\sum_k c_k x^{q_k}$ with distinct real exponents, to which Laguerre's
  extension of Descartes' rule of signs applies~\cite{Jameson2006}: the
  number of positive zeros, with multiplicity, is at most $V$ and has
  its parity. The parity statement also follows from the signs of $D$
  as $\gamma \to \mp\infty$, which are those of the first and last nonzero $c_k$
  (by the termwise argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma_eq_0_or_inf},
  applied to $D$).
  \end{proof}

  The hypothesis $D \not\equiv 0$ excludes in particular graphs with
  equal degree-pair profiles, which are tied for every $\gamma$. The
  sign-change bound $V$ can be attained: two trees of order $12$ with
  maximum degree $6$ and eight distinct imbalances, with $V = 5$, cross
  five times at $(\alpha,\beta) = (-1,0)$ (crossings counted as sign
  changes of $D$ on a fine $\gamma$ grid; in that numerical search they are
  the only such pair among the order-$12$ trees, whereas the bound itself
  is analytic); whether five crossings are attained for $\Delta \le 4$ is
  open. The proposition concerns the $\gamma$ axis
  only: along other directions the exponents are not the $q_k$ and the
  constant five does not apply.

  Finally, the per-graph identifiability noted in
  Remark~\ref{rem:ident} can be stated exactly. Let $R(G)$ be the set of
  degree pairs realised by $G$, $r(G)$ the number of distinct imbalances
  among them, and $A(G)$ the affine dimension of
  $\{z_{ij} : (i,j) \in R(G)\}$.

  \begin{proposition}[Per-graph scale-invariant directions]\label{prop:identrank}
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] $A(G)$ equals the rank of the Hessian
  $\operatorname{Cov}_{\theta}(z)$ of $\log LO_G$ at every $\theta$, and
  the directions $v$ with $LO(G;\theta + tv) = e^{ct}\,LO(G;\theta)$ for
  all $t$ and $\theta$ form a subspace of dimension $3 - A(G)$.
  \item[(ii)] $A(G) \le \min\bigl(|R(G)| - 1,\ r(G),\ 3\bigr)$ for every
  graph.
  \item[(iii)] If $r(G) = 2$ with imbalances $q_1 \neq q_2$, then
  $v = (1, -2, c)$ with
  $c = [\ln(1 - q_2^2) - \ln(1 - q_1^2)]/(q_1 - q_2)$ is such a
  direction.
  \end{enumerate}
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
  (i) is the standard minimal-representation property of exponential
  families~\cite{Brown1986}: $\operatorname{Cov}_{\theta}(z)$ has full
  support on $R(G)$, so its rank is the affine dimension of the support,
  and $v^{\top} z$ is constant on $R(G)$ exactly for $v$ orthogonal to
  the differences of the $z_{ij}$. (ii) By~\eqref{eq:factor},
  $z_{ij} = (2s + \ln(1-q^2) - \ln 4,\ s,\ q)$ with $s = \ln(i+j)$, so
  pairs with the same imbalance lie on one line parallel to $(2,1,0)$,
  and $r$ parallel lines span an affine space of dimension at most $r$.
  (iii) $v^{\top} z_{ij} = \ln(1 - q_{ij}^2) - \ln 4 + c\,q_{ij}$ takes
  the same value at $q_1$ and $q_2$ by the choice of $c$.
  \end{proof}

  Equality holds in~(ii) for every set of admissible pairs with
  $\Delta \le 6$ (exhaustive check in $60$-digit arithmetic), hence for
  all molecular graphs; it fails for some pair sets from $\Delta = 7$,
  e.g.\ $\{(1,7),(2,6),(3,5),(4,4)\}$; every failing set we found has a
  constant degree sum, which no connected graph realises with four
  distinct pairs.
  For example, the spider with four legs of length three realises the
  pairs $(2,4)$, $(2,2)$ and $(1,2)$, which have only two imbalances
  ($\tfrac13$ and $0$); on it, $\gamma$ is confounded with
  $(\alpha,\beta)$ along the direction of part~(iii).
  For a QSPR dataset the relevant set is the union of the pairs realised
  by its molecules, since only a direction along which every molecule is
  rescaled by the same factor leaves correlations unchanged; on the
  octanes this union contains nine of the ten admissible pairs and its
  affine dimension is $3$, so no parameter direction is confounded.

  \section{Bounds for the Loyola family}\label{sec:bounds}
  % =====================================================================
  We give a bracket bound via the edge-contribution function $\psi$,
  bounds in $(m, \Delta, \delta)$ for non-negative parameters, a
  Cauchy--Schwarz interpolation bound, a uniform ratio bound, and the
  $\gamma \to \pm\infty$ asymptotics; the $\gamma \neq 0$ bounds rest on
  bounding $e^{\gamma q_{uv}}$ edge by edge (for $\gamma \ge 0$) between $1$ and
  $e^{\gamma(\Delta-1)/(\Delta+1)}$. Throughout, $G$ is a simple
  connected graph with $\delta \le d_v \le \Delta$ for every
  $v \in V(G)$. Several of the inequalities have been machine-checked
  (Reproducibility paragraph, Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).


  \begin{remark}[Attribution at $\gamma = 0$]\label{rem:gmattr}
  At $\gamma = 0$ the family coincides with $M_{\alpha,\beta}$, for
  which inequalities of all the types developed here are already known:
  $(\Delta,\delta,m)$-bounds with regular/biregular equality and
  two-sided comparisons against $M_1$, $M_2$, $R$, $\chi$, $H$, $GA$,
  $AG$ and $ISI$~\cite{MolinaSigarreta2025GM}, Jensen- and H\"older-type
  relations~\cite{GranadosJMC2025GM}, and further
  inequalities~\cite{PortillaJMC2025GM}. The $\gamma = 0$
  specializations below are therefore not claimed as new; the content
  of this section is their extension to $\gamma \neq 0$, where the
  imbalance factor $e^{\gamma q_{uv}}$ enters every edge weight, and the
  tracking of how $\gamma$ deforms the equality cases (the
  $\gamma = 0$ comparisons with the Randi\'c, sum-connectivity and
  Sombor indices are not repeated).
  \end{remark}


  The summand in~\eqref{eq:wuzi} depends only on the unordered
  edge degree pair $(d_u, d_v)$. We isolate this dependence as follows:

  \begin{definition}\label{def:psi}
  For real parameters $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ and positive reals
  $x, y$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:psi}
  \edgepsi = (xy)^{\alpha}(x + y)^{\beta}
  \exp\!\left(\gamma\,\tfrac{|x - y|}{x + y}\right).
  \end{equation}
  \end{definition}

  With this notation $\Wuzi = \sum_{uv \in E(G)} \psi(d_u, d_v; \alpha,
  \beta, \gamma)$.

  \begin{proposition}\label{thm:bracket}
  Let $G$ be a simple connected graph of size $m \ge 1$ with
  degrees in $[\delta, \Delta]$. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:bracket}
  m \cdot \psi_{\min}(\delta, \Delta; \alpha, \beta, \gamma)
  \;\le\; \Wuzi \;\le\;
  m \cdot \psi_{\max}(\delta, \Delta; \alpha, \beta, \gamma),
  \end{equation}
  where,
  $\psi_{\min} = \min\{\psi(i, j; \alpha, \beta, \gamma)
                : \delta \le i \le j \le \Delta\}$
  and $\psi_{\max} = \max\{\psi(i, j; \alpha, \beta, \gamma)
                : \delta \le i \le j \le \Delta\}$. The upper
  inequality is an equality if and only if every edge of $G$ has a
  degree pair attaining $\psi_{\max}$, and the lower
  inequality is an equality if and only if every edge has a degree
  pair attaining $\psi_{\min}$.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
  Every edge $uv$ has its degree pair in the admissible set
  $\mathcal{P}(\delta, \Delta) := \{(i, j) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : \delta \le
  i \le j \le \Delta\}$, so
  $\psi_{\min} \le \psi(d_u, d_v; \alpha, \beta, \gamma) \le \psi_{\max}$
  on every edge; summing over the $m$ edges and using
  $\sum_{uv} \psi(d_u, d_v; \alpha, \beta, \gamma) = \Wuzi$
  gives~\eqref{eq:bracket}. Each side of~\eqref{eq:bracket} is the sum
  of $m$ per-edge inequalities, so it is an equality if and only if
  every per-edge inequality is, i.e.\ if and only if every edge has a
  degree pair attaining $\psi_{\max}$ (upper side), respectively
  $\psi_{\min}$ (lower side), over $\mathcal{P}(\delta, \Delta)$.
  \end{proof}


  \begin{corollary}\label{thm:cs}
  Let $\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 = 2\alpha$, $\beta_1 + \beta_2 = 2\beta$,
  and $\gamma_1 + \gamma_2 = 2\gamma$. Then for every simple
  connected graph $G$ without isolated vertices,
  \[
  LO(G; \alpha, \beta, \gamma)
  \;\le\;
  \sqrt{LO(G; \alpha_1, \beta_1, \gamma_1)
         \cdot LO(G; \alpha_2, \beta_2, \gamma_2)}
  \]
  Equality holds if and only if the ratio
  \[
  \psi(d_u, d_v; \alpha_1, \beta_1, \gamma_1)/\psi(d_u, d_v; \alpha_2,
  \beta_2, \gamma_2)
  \]
  is the same for every edge of $G$.
  \end{corollary}
  \begin{proof}
  For each edge $e = uv$ write
  $\psi_e^{(k)} := \psi(d_u, d_v; \alpha_k, \beta_k, \gamma_k) > 0$,
  $k \in \{1, 2\}$. By additivity of the exponents and the hypotheses on
  $(\alpha_k, \beta_k, \gamma_k)$,
  $\psi_e^{(1)}\psi_e^{(2)} = \psi(d_u, d_v; \alpha, \beta, \gamma)^2$
  on every edge. The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality for
  $a_e = (\psi_e^{(1)})^{1/2}$ and $b_e = (\psi_e^{(2)})^{1/2}$ gives
  $\sum_e a_e b_e \le (\sum_e a_e^2)^{1/2} (\sum_e b_e^2)^{1/2}$, which
  is the claim because $\sum_e a_e b_e = \Wuzi$,
  $\sum_e a_e^2 = LO(G; \alpha_1, \beta_1, \gamma_1)$ and
  $\sum_e b_e^2 = LO(G; \alpha_2, \beta_2, \gamma_2)$. Equality holds if
  and only if $b_e = \lambda a_e$ for a constant $\lambda \ge 0$ and all
  $e$, i.e.\ if and only if
  $\psi_e^{(2)}/\psi_e^{(1)} = \lambda^2$ is the same on every edge.
  (The inequality itself is midpoint log-convexity of
  $\theta \mapsto LO(G; \theta)$, Theorem~\ref{thm:logconvex}.)
  \end{proof}


  \begin{proposition}\label{thm:upper_Delta}
  Let $G$ be a connected graph of size $m$ and maximum degree $\Delta$. For
  $\alpha, \beta \ge 0$ and $\gamma \ge 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:upper_Delta}
  \Wuzi \;\le\; m \cdot \Delta^{2\alpha} \cdot (2\Delta)^{\beta}
  \cdot \exp\!\left(\gamma\,\frac{\Delta - 1}{\Delta + 1}\right).
  \end{equation}
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
  On every edge $uv$ we have $1 \le d_u, d_v \le \Delta$, hence
  $d_u d_v \le \Delta^2$ and $d_u + d_v \le 2\Delta$; and the normalised
  imbalance $\mathrm{imb}(x, y) = |x - y|/(x + y)$ is maximised over
  $\{(i, j) : 1 \le i \le j \le \Delta\}$ at $(1, \Delta)$, so
  $|d_u - d_v|/(d_u + d_v) \le (\Delta - 1)/(\Delta + 1)$. Since
  $\alpha, \beta, \gamma \ge 0$, the maps $t \mapsto t^{\alpha}$,
  $t \mapsto t^{\beta}$ and $t \mapsto e^{\gamma t}$ are non-decreasing,
  so $\psi(d_u, d_v; \alpha, \beta, \gamma) \le \Delta^{2\alpha}
  (2\Delta)^{\beta} \exp(\gamma(\Delta - 1)/(\Delta + 1))$; summing over
  the $m$ edges yields~\eqref{eq:upper_Delta}. For $\gamma > 0$
  and $\Delta \ge 2$ the bound is attained if and only if
  $\alpha = \beta = 0$ and every edge has degree pair $(1, \Delta)$
  (for connected $G$, $G \cong K_{1,\Delta}$); if $\alpha > 0$ or
  $\beta > 0$ the three factor-wise maxima are not simultaneously
  attainable and the inequality is strict.
  \end{proof}


  \begin{proposition}\label{thm:lower_delta}
  Let $G$ be a connected graph of size $m$ and minimum degree $\delta$. For
  $\alpha, \beta \ge 0$ and $\gamma \ge 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:lower_delta}
  \Wuzi \;\ge\; m \cdot \delta^{2\alpha} \cdot (2\delta)^{\beta},
  \end{equation}
  with equality if and only if $G$ is $\delta$-regular, except in the
  degenerate case $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) = (0, 0, 0)$, where
  $\Wuzi \equiv m$ and the bound holds with equality for every graph.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
  On every edge $d_u, d_v \ge \delta$, so for $\alpha, \beta \ge 0$,
  $(d_u d_v)^{\alpha} \ge \delta^{2\alpha}$ and
  $(d_u + d_v)^{\beta} \ge (2\delta)^{\beta}$, and for $\gamma \ge 0$ the
  exponential factor is at least $1$ because the imbalance is
  non-negative. Multiplying and summing over the $m$ edges
  yields~\eqref{eq:lower_delta}. If $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) = (0, 0, 0)$
  then $LO(G; 0, 0, 0) = m$ equals the bound for every graph. Otherwise,
  equality requires equality in every active per-edge factor: if
  $\alpha > 0$ or $\beta > 0$ this forces $d_u = d_v = \delta$ on every
  edge; if $\alpha = \beta = 0$ and $\gamma > 0$ it forces $d_u = d_v$
  on every edge, so the connected graph $G$ is regular, hence
  $\delta$-regular. Conversely, on a $\delta$-regular graph every edge
  has degree pair $(\delta, \delta)$ and equality holds.
  \end{proof}

  The next proposition gives a single uniform template for bounding
  $\Wuzi$ both above and below in terms of any classical
  bond-incident-degree index whose edge contribution is strictly
  positive on the admissible degree pairs of the graph.

  \begin{proposition}\label{thm:ratio_bound}
  Let $G$ be a simple graph with maximum degree at most $\Delta$, and let
  $I_h(G) = \sum_{uv \in E(G)} h(d_u, d_v)$ be a BID index with
  edge contribution $h(i, j)$ strictly positive on every degree pair
  $(i, j)$ that appears in $G$. Define
  \[
  c_{\min}^{h} = \min_{(i, j)}\,\frac{\psi(i, j; \alpha, \beta,
  \gamma)}{h(i, j)},
  \qquad
  c_{\max}^{h} = \max_{(i, j)}\,\frac{\psi(i, j; \alpha, \beta,
  \gamma)}{h(i, j)},
  \]
  where the extrema range over the degree pairs actually appearing in
  $G$.
  Then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio_bound}
  c_{\min}^{h} \cdot I_h(G)
  \;\le\; \Wuzi
  \;\le\; c_{\max}^{h} \cdot I_h(G),
  \end{equation}
  with equality on either side if and only if every edge of $G$
  attains the corresponding extremum of $\psi/h$.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
  Since $h(d_u, d_v) > 0$ and $c_{\min}^{h} \le \psi(d_u, d_v; \alpha,
  \beta, \gamma)/h(d_u, d_v) \le c_{\max}^{h}$ on every edge $uv$,
  multiplying by $h(d_u, d_v)$ and summing over $E(G)$, with
  $\sum_{uv} h(d_u, d_v) = I_h(G)$ and $\sum_{uv} \psi = \Wuzi$,
  gives~\eqref{eq:ratio_bound}; each side is an equality if and only if
  every edge attains the corresponding extremum of $\psi/h$ over the
  appearing pairs.
  \end{proof}


  Proposition~\ref{thm:ratio_bound} is a \emph{sharp graph-specific}
  comparison: its constants depend on the degree pairs that actually
  appear in $G$. A uniform version follows immediately by enlarging
  the range of the extrema.

  \begin{corollary}[Uniform $\Delta$-bounded version]\label{cor:ratio_uniform}
  With the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{thm:ratio_bound}, but with $h$
  strictly positive on all of
  $P_{\Delta} = \{(i,j) : 1 \le i \le j \le \Delta\}$ and the extrema
  taken over $P_{\Delta}$,
  inequality~\eqref{eq:ratio_bound} holds simultaneously for every
  graph of maximum degree at most $\Delta$, with constants independent
  of the individual graph. Equality on either side holds if and only
  if every edge of $G$ realises a degree pair attaining the
  corresponding extremum over $P_{\Delta}$; the uniform constants are
  in general strictly worse than the graph-specific ones, since the
  extrema are taken over a superset.
  \end{corollary}


  \begin{proposition}\label{prop:gamma_eq_0_or_inf}
  At $\gamma = 0$, $LO(G; \alpha, \beta, 0)$ coincides with the
  two-parameter $(\alpha, \beta)$ degree-based index
  $\sum_{uv \in E(G)} (d_u d_v)^{\alpha}(d_u + d_v)^{\beta}$.
  Let $E_{\max}(G)$ denote the set of edges realising
  $\mathrm{imb}_{\max}(G) = \max_{uv} \mathrm{imb}(d_u, d_v)$
  and $E_{\min}(G)$ the set realising
  $\mathrm{imb}_{\min}(G) = \min_{uv} \mathrm{imb}(d_u, d_v)$.
  Then as $\gamma \to +\infty$,
  \[
  \frac{\Wuzi}{e^{\gamma\, \mathrm{imb}_{\max}(G)}}
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  \sum_{uv \in E_{\max}(G)} (d_u d_v)^{\alpha}(d_u + d_v)^{\beta},
  \]
  and as $\gamma \to -\infty$,
  \[
  \Wuzi / e^{\gamma\, \mathrm{imb}_{\min}(G)} \longrightarrow
  \sum_{uv \in E_{\min}(G)} (d_u d_v)^{\alpha}(d_u + d_v)^{\beta}.
  \]
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
  At $\gamma = 0$ the exponential factor is $1$ on every edge. With
  $\mu(G) := \mathrm{imb}_{\max}(G)$ and
  $\varepsilon_{uv} := \mu(G) - \mathrm{imb}(d_u, d_v) \ge 0$,
  $\Wuzi / e^{\gamma \mu(G)} = \sum_{uv} (d_u d_v)^{\alpha}(d_u +
  d_v)^{\beta} e^{-\gamma \varepsilon_{uv}}$; the summands over
  $E_{\max}(G)$ ($\varepsilon_{uv} = 0$) do not depend on $\gamma$, and
  the others ($\varepsilon_{uv} > 0$) tend to $0$ as $\gamma \to
  +\infty$, termwise since $E(G)$ is finite. The case
  $\gamma \to -\infty$ is dual.
  \end{proof}

\subsection{The degree-pair ceiling}\label{sec:ceiling}

The \emph{ceiling} of this subsection and the \emph{floor} of the
next are the same limit seen from two sides: no vertex-degree-based
index can separate graphs with equal degree-pair counts, so the number
of distinct degree-pair profiles bounds the number of distinct values
(equivalently, the minimum attainable degeneracy).

  \begin{proposition}[Degree-pair information ceiling]\label{prop:collision}
  If two graphs $G$ and $H$ have identical unordered
  edge-degree-pair count vectors, $m_{ij}(G) = m_{ij}(H)$ for all
  $i \le j$, then $LO(G;\theta) = LO(H;\theta)$ for every
  $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and more generally
  $TI(G) = TI(H)$ for every vertex-degree-based index $TI$. Among the
  $18$ octane isomers exactly $16$ distinct count vectors occur: the
  pairs $\{$3-methylheptane, 4-methylheptane$\}$ and
  $\{$3,4-dimethylhexane, 3-ethyl-2-methylpentane$\}$ share their
  count vectors while differing in measured properties
  (Table~\ref{tab:collisions}), so every vertex-degree-based
  descriptor, tuned or not, carries a nonzero irreducible error on
  this benchmark.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
  By Definition~\ref{def:wuzi}, $LO(G;\theta)$ depends on $G$ only
  through the multiset of unordered edge degree pairs, i.e.\ through
  $(m_{ij}(G))_{i \le j}$; the same holds for every vertex-degree-based
  index~\cite{Rada2026LinearVDB}. The counts for the $18$ octane
  isomers are computed and cross-checked in the reproducibility
  package; both collision pairs have their common count vectors listed
  in Table~\ref{tab:collisions}.
  \end{proof}

  The ceiling is tight in the following sense: distinct count vectors
  are separated by the family at almost every parameter choice.

  \begin{proposition}[Generic discrimination of distinct
  profiles]\label{prop:generic}
  Fix $\Delta \ge 1$ and let $m \neq m'$ be two distinct
  edge-degree-pair count vectors supported on the admissible pairs
  $P_{\Delta}$. Then
  $D(\theta) = \sum_{(i,j) \in P_{\Delta}} (m_{ij} - m'_{ij})\,
  e^{\theta^{\top} z_{ij}}$ is a real-analytic function of $\theta$
  that is not identically zero, and its zero set has Lebesgue measure
  zero in $\mathbb{R}^3$. Consequently, any two graphs with distinct
  count vectors are distinguished by $LO(\cdot\,;\theta)$ for almost
  every $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^3$; only graphs with identical count
  vectors (Proposition~\ref{prop:collision}) are indistinguishable
  for all $\theta$.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
  The map $(i,j) \mapsto z_{ij}$ is injective on $P_{\Delta}$, since
  $\ln(ij)$ and $\ln(i+j)$ recover the product and the sum, which
  determine the unordered pair. Choose $v \in \mathbb{R}^3$ such that
  the inner products $v^{\top} z_{ij}$ are pairwise distinct (this
  excludes finitely many hyperplanes of $v$'s). Along the ray
  $\theta = t v$, $D(tv) = \sum_k c_k e^{t \lambda_k}$ with distinct
  exponents $\lambda_k$ and coefficients
  $c_k = m_{i_kj_k} - m'_{i_kj_k}$, not all zero; such an exponential
  sum vanishes identically only if all $c_k = 0$, so $D \not\equiv 0$
  on the ray and hence on $\mathbb{R}^3$. $D$ is a finite sum of
  exponentials of linear functionals, hence real-analytic on
  $\mathbb{R}^3$, and the zero set of a nonzero real-analytic
  function has Lebesgue measure zero.
  \end{proof}

  The proof uses only the injectivity of
  $(i,j) \mapsto (\ln(ij), \ln(i+j))$, so the same conclusion already
  holds for the parent family $M_{\alpha,\beta}$ on the
  $(\alpha,\beta)$-plane. Proposition~\ref{prop:generic} is therefore
  a property the Loyola family inherits, not an advantage conferred by
  $\gamma$. Likewise, since
  $q_{ij} = \sqrt{1 - 4ij/(i+j)^{2}}$ is a function of the pair, the
  imbalance coordinate adds no graph information beyond the
  edge-degree-pair counts (every vertex-degree-based index is a
  functional of those counts~\cite{Rada2026LinearVDB}); what $\gamma$
  adds is the new functional direction of
  Lemma~\ref{prop:indep}, i.e.\ a different weighting of the
  same information.

\subsection{First loss of discrimination across tree orders}\label{sec:degeneracy}

Degeneracy measures an index's ability to discriminate non-isomorphic
graphs~\cite{Konstantinova1996Degeneracy}; lower is better. On the
$106$ non-isomorphic trees of order $10$, with the distinct-value
convention $100(1 - k/N)$ ($k$ distinct values among $N = 106$ trees;
Supplementary Section~S5), the pure-$\gamma$ points $LO(0, 0, 1)$ and
$LO(0, 0, 2)$ attain degeneracy $25.5\%$ ($79$ distinct values), tied
with $R$, $GA$ and $AG$; since the $106$ trees realise only $79$ distinct
edge-degree-pair count vectors, $25.5\%$ is the minimum attainable by
\emph{any} degree-pair-based index on this set, while $M_1$ ($83.0\%$)
and $M_2$ ($67.0\%$) are the most degenerate (Supplementary
Figure~S2). Repeating the computation for all trees of
orders $7$ through $12$ and for their molecular subsets with
$\Delta \le 4$ (Supplementary Table~S4), the pure-$\gamma$ points attain
the degree-pair-profile floor at every order in both sets; so does the
Randi\'c index (while $\chi$ falls off the floor from order $10$, with
$77$ values against $79$ profiles), so
floor attainment distinguishes the pure-$\gamma$ points from $M_1$ and
$M_2$ but not from $R$. Theorem~\ref{thm:ratioplane} turns this into an
exact statement: the pure-$\gamma$ points separate precisely the
$q$-histograms, which coincide with the degree-pair profiles up to
order $12$ (order $15$ for $\Delta \le 4$) and not beyond. Among the
trees of order $13$ the profiles
$\{(1,2)^{1}, (1,3)^{2}, (1,6)^{5}, (2,3)^{2}, (2,6)^{1}, (3,3)^{1}\}$
and $\{(1,3)^{3}, (1,6)^{5}, (2,2)^{1}, (2,3)^{2}, (3,6)^{1}\}$
(exponents are edge multiplicities) share the $q$-histogram
$\{0^{1}, \tfrac15^{2}, \tfrac13^{1}, \tfrac12^{3}, \tfrac57^{5}\}$.
Table~\ref{tab:firstfail} collects the first failures; at order $14$,
where $R$ falls below the floor, the pure-$\gamma$ points give $1090$
values, and $R$ still attains the floor at order $13$ ($570$ values).

Off the ratio plane the imbalance factor has a visible effect at fixed
classical exponents. A point $(0,\beta,0)$ weights an edge by its degree
sum only, so two degree pairs with the same sum receive the same
weight; with $\gamma \neq 0$ they are separated, because their
imbalances differ. Computationally, on all trees of order $16$
($4069$ profiles), $LO(0,\tfrac12,0)$ and $LO(0,\tfrac13,0)$ take only
$3475$ and $3476$ distinct values, whereas $LO(0,\tfrac12,1)$ and
$LO(0,\tfrac13,1)$ separate all $4069$ profiles; likewise
$LO(\tfrac12,0,\cdot)$ rises from $3940$ to $4067$ and the Randi\'c point
$LO(-\tfrac12,0,\cdot)$ from $4007$ to $4068$ when $\gamma$ goes from $0$
to $1$, while points on $\Pi$ such as $GA$ are unaffected ($3996$ in
both cases), as Theorem~\ref{thm:ratioplane} requires. The effect is
one of repairing particular classical exponents, not of making
discrimination possible at all: generic parent points such as
$LO(0.3,0.2,0)$ already separate all
$4069$ profiles (Proposition~\ref{prop:generic}). It is established by
enumeration for orders $12$--$16$ rather than proved; it does not translate into a
predictive gain over a tuned parent (Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}),
although at the same fixed fractional exponents $\gamma = 1$ improves
the leave-one-out $Q^2$ of $\gamma = 0$ in $52$ of $81$
point--dataset combinations (exploratory; Supplementary Section~S5).

\subsection{Structure sensitivity on decane isomers}\label{sec:ss}

The structure-sensitivity criterion of Furtula, Gutman and
Dehmer~\cite{FurtulaGutmanDehmer2013SS} (with a fingerprint-based
variant due to Raki\'c and Furtula~\cite{RakicFurtula2019SS}) was
implemented with the published protocol on the $75$ decane isomer
trees, the similar structures of a tree being those obtained by
relocating a single edge within the molecular class; $SS$ and $Abr$
are the class means of the mean and the maximum relative change of the
index over these neighbours. The implementation reproduces the
published decane-class values of Barman and Das~\cite{BarmanDas2026HSO}
(eleven of fourteen $SS$/$Abr$ values exactly at the four displayed
decimals, the other three within one unit in the fourth); the protocol
and this control are detailed in Supplementary Section~S6. Under the
published desideratum ($SS$ large, $Abr$ small), $LO(0,0,2)$ has a
structure sensitivity equal to that of the hyper-Zagreb index
($0.1257$ against $0.1256$) at a markedly smaller abruptness ($0.274$
against $0.313$), and the sensitivity-to-abruptness ratios of the
pure-$\gamma$ points ($0.4579$ and $0.4565$) are comparable to those of
$H/2$, $GA$ and $R$ ($0.448$--$0.451$; Table~\ref{tab:fgdss}); in view
of the redundancy noted in Section~\ref{sec:octanes}, these
third-decimal differences are not a ranking. Moreover, $SS$ is not a
fixed property of the family: along the pure-$\gamma$ line it grows
with $\gamma$ ($0.031$, $0.062$, $0.126$, $0.319$ at
$\gamma = 0.5, 1, 2, 5$) while the ratio peaks near $\gamma = 2$ and
then falls ($0.427$ at $\gamma = 5$); and the parameter-free aggregate
$IRLA$, which the pure-$\gamma$ points approximate to first order in
$\gamma$, has a higher ratio ($SS = 0.2343$, $Abr = 0.5066$, ratio
$0.4625$). The favourable sensitivity--abruptness balance is therefore
a property of linear imbalance weighting rather than of the
exponential form, and the equality of $SS$ with hyper-Zagreb at
$\gamma = 2$ reflects the chosen value of $\gamma$; it is a tie, not a
ranking.

\section{A concise QSPR diagnostic}\label{sec:sensitivity}
% =====================================================================

We benchmark the Loyola family against the classical indices it
reduces to on the $18$ octane isomers, following the chemometric
benchmark of the diminished Sombor
study~\cite{MovahediGutmanRedzepovicFurtula2026}, and on the $35$
nonane isomers as a replication set (models refitted on it). The comparison set is restricted to
the exact reductions of Section~\ref{sec:intro} ---
$M_1, M_2, HM, {}^{m}\!M_2, R, \chi, H/2, ISI, GA, AG$ --- together
with the two fixed pure-$\gamma$ benchmark points $LO(0, 0, 1)$
and $LO(0, 0, 2)$ chosen for this study (the edge count
$m = LO(0, 0, 0)$ is constant on the octanes and omitted); no parameter
in the correlation comparison is tuned against the property data (an
exploratory local-tuning check, Supplementary Section~S2, did not exceed
the best fixed benchmark except by a $0.001$ in-sample
$\Delta H_{\mathrm{vap}}$ margin). All comparisons are descriptive and diagnostic:
on these small sets they show what imbalance information does and does
not add to the parent family, not chemical predictive utility, and
given the number of properties, indices, and tuning candidates
examined, no confirmatory inference is claimed. Values are computed in
double precision and rounded \emph{once} for display (three decimals
for correlations, $Q^2$ and RMSE, four for structure sensitivity), so a
tabulated difference may differ by one unit in the last place from the
difference of two displayed entries.

\subsection{Property correlations on the octane isomers}\label{sec:octanes}

Table~\ref{tab:lo_octane} reports the Pearson correlation $r$ between
each index and each of the five physicochemical properties of the
$18$ octane isomers (boiling point $T_B$, enthalpy of formation
$\Delta H_f$, enthalpy of vaporization $\Delta H_{\mathrm{vap}}$,
entropy $S$, acentric factor $\omega$; data and units in
Appendix~\ref{app:data}). Property values are traced
to the NIST WebBook~\cite{NISTWebBook}, except the acentric factors
(KDB-derived compilations; NIST does not list them); all $90$
molecule-level values were audited against authoritative sources
(Supplementary Section~S1, with the molecule-by-molecule discussion).
Three caveats remain. The $298$\,K vaporization enthalpy of
2,2,3,3-tetramethylbutane, a solid at $298$\,K (triple point
$374$\,K), is the benchmark-compilation value $8.41$\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$
($35.19$\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$), matching neither NIST standard vaporization
enthalpy ($42.94$ and $42.91$\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$, about
$10.26$\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$), so its physical reference state is
unresolved. Six gas-phase entropies could not be verified against a
primary source, and four entropies differ from a widely used
compilation; substituting the compilation values changes all twelve

\subsection{Freeing $\gamma$ gives no consistent gain once the parent is
not truncated}\label{sec:ablation}

Given identical search budgets, does freeing $\gamma$ improve
out-of-sample prediction beyond an optimized parent $M_{\alpha,\beta}$?
In a fully nested leave-one-out design (details in Supplementary
Section~S3), for each property and each outer fold, candidate parameter
vectors drawn uniformly per coordinate from a search box (triples
$(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ for the Loyola model, pairs $(\alpha,\beta)$
with $\gamma = 0$ for the parent; identical budget) are scored on the
training isomers only by \emph{inner} leave-one-out root-mean-square
error of the one-descriptor linear regression; the best candidate is
refit and the held-out isomer predicted, so no quantity computed on the
held-out isomer enters selection or fitting. In the box $(-2,2)$ used
first, freeing $\gamma$ appeared to help the vaporization enthalpy
(Supplementary Table~S3): over one hundred candidate streams it improved
nested predictive performance for $\Delta H_{\mathrm{vap}}$ in every
seed ($100/100$ at budgets $200$ and $500$; median
$\Delta Q^2 = +0.069$ and $+0.068$), while it usually degraded
$\Delta H_f$ and gave mixed or slightly negative results for the other
properties (Supplementary Section~S3 also reports the analysis with
2,2,3,3-tetramethylbutane excluded). These counts describe optimizer variance on one fixed set of $18$ molecules
and carry no inferential weight, and they hide a boundary effect: for
$\Delta H_{\mathrm{vap}}$ the parent chose
$(\alpha,\beta) = (1.021, -1.993)$, on the boundary of the box, in $17$
of the $18$ folds.

These comparisons share one design choice that is decisive: the box
$(-2,2)$, which the nonane pre-registration also fixed. Because $\ln(ij) = 2\ln(i+j) +
\ln(1-q^2) - \ln 4$ (equation~\eqref{eq:factor}), the parent family
reaches imbalance information through $(1-q^2)^{\alpha}$ along the
direction $\beta \approx -2\alpha$, but it needs large exponents to
weight it strongly, and the box truncates exactly that direction. To
test this, the matched comparison was repeated (this analysis was
not pre-registered) with $2000$
candidates per model, seeds $0$--$49$, and boxes of half-width $2$
and $12$ for both models (Table~\ref{tab:box}). In the box of
half-width $12$ the parent's selections are interior in more than
$90\%$ of folds for every property, and the vaporization-enthalpy gain
shrinks to about zero: a hybrid control that widens only
$(\alpha,\beta)$ gives median $\Delta Q^2 = +0.004$ on the octanes
($28$ of $50$ seeds) and $-0.002$ on the nonanes ($24$ of $50$), and
widening all three coordinates gives $-0.011$ and $-0.016$. On the
other properties no advantage is consistent across boxes: under the
hybrid control the Loyola model is ahead for octane $S$ ($38$ of $50$
seeds) and nonane $T_B$ ($31$ of $50$), but behind for octane $T_B$,
$\Delta H_f$ and $\omega$, and the wide box itself lowers the parent's
$Q^2$ for $S$ and $T_B$. The vaporization-enthalpy gains reported above
therefore reflect the truncated box rather than information that the
parent lacks. The economy that remains belongs to linear imbalance
weighting rather than to $\gamma$: the fixed point $LO(0,0,1)$ and the
parameter-free $IRLA$ approach what the parent reaches only near
$(\alpha,\beta) \approx (4.5, -9)$ ($Q^2 = 0.957$ and $0.948$ against
$0.949$ there, on the octanes).

\subsection{Replication on the nonane isomers and stronger
baselines}\label{sec:external}

Under a protocol committed to the reproducibility package before any
nonane value was collected, the identical nested design was applied to
the $35$ constitutional nonane isomers, with boiling points ($35$ NIST
averages of experimental determinations) and standard vaporization
enthalpies ($34$ values) from the NIST WebBook~\cite{NISTWebBook}; only $5$ of the $34$ vaporization
enthalpies are calorimetric determinations and $29$ come from two
compilations~\cite{LabaufGreenshieldsRossini1961, WilhoitZwolinski1971},
so agreement with them measures consistency with the compiled values
rather than independent experimental validation; within-fold
baselines are in Table~\ref{tab:external}. The pre-registered verdicts are that the
$\gamma$ effect \emph{replicates} for $\Delta H_{\mathrm{vap}}$
(compilation values; LO better than GM in $95/100$ and $98/100$ seeds,
median $\Delta Q^2 = +0.048$ and $+0.053$) and is \emph{mixed} for $T_B$
($58/100$ and $63/100$). The pre-registered protocol, however, fixed the
box of half-width $2$, which binds the parent (Table~\ref{tab:box}); in
a box that does not, the nonane gain disappears (median
$\Delta Q^2 = -0.002$ under the hybrid control), and the pre-registered
verdict is reported as obtained, with this deviation recorded. The
fixed point $LO(0,0,1)$, which involves no tuning at all, matches or
beats the tuned Loyola model on both sets ($0.957$ and $0.903$ against
medians $0.953$ and $0.900$). It is essentially $IRLA$:
$LO(G;0,0,\gamma) = m + \tfrac{\gamma}{2}\,IRLA(G) + O(\gamma^2)$, the
correlation between the two is $0.996$ on both sets, and $IRLA$ itself
comes within $0.003$ of the median Loyola model on the nonanes ($0.897$
against $0.900$), so what matters for this property is a term linear in
the imbalance, not the tuned exponential. Ridge regression on the full
degree-pair count vector predicts the nonanes better than every index
model ($Q^2 = 0.927$ for $\Delta H_{\mathrm{vap}}$, $0.682$ for $T_B$),
consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:collision} and the remark after
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic}: every index in the family is a
one-dimensional reweighting of the degree-pair counts, and the imbalance
coordinate re-weights that information rather than adding to it. Protocol
details, an exploratory transfer analysis and a post hoc molecule
bootstrap (its $95\%$ interval for LO minus GM on nonane
$\Delta H_{\mathrm{vap}}$, $[-0.006, +0.113]$ at budget $200$, includes
zero) are in Supplementary Section~S4.

\subsection{Measured boiling points: decanes and pooled C6--C10}\label{sec:bp}

Because measured vaporization enthalpies are too scarce for a larger
test (NIST lists calorimetric $298$\,K values for only $25$ acyclic
alkane skeletons outside $C_8$ and $C_9$, and for one of the $75$
decanes), two further tests used measured boiling points, under a
protocol committed to the repository before any model was fitted (the
NIST pages had been archived two days earlier during a
data-availability audit, which counted available values only). Boiling points were taken from the NIST
WebBook~\cite{NISTWebBook} by a fixed rule (the NIST average, or else
the median of non-compilation determinations if they agree within
$3$\,K; otherwise the molecule is excluded), giving all hexanes, heptanes and octanes, $34$ of the $35$ nonanes
(one has two NIST entries with the same InChI whose boiling points differ
by $6.4$\,K) and $34$ of the $75$ decanes ($31$ have no NIST boiling point
and $10$ have determinations more than $3$\,K apart; for $14$ of the $34$ included decanes NIST lists a single
non-compilation determination, so the agreement rule does not apply to
them). Because the same rule is applied to every molecule, the octane
boiling points in the pooled set can differ from those of
Table~\ref{tab:octane-data} by up to $0.5\,^{\circ}$C (for example
2,4-dimethylhexane, $108.9$ against $109.4\,^{\circ}$C, a documented
source variation). The included and excluded decanes do not differ detectably in
branching or imbalance (numbers of tertiary and quaternary carbons and
of methyl groups, Wiener index, $IRLA$, $LO(0,0,1)$ and $R$; two-sided
permutation tests, all $p \ge 0.10$), although with $34$ and $41$
molecules such tests can rule out only large differences. Test~A evaluates the models on the decanes as a new cohort, refitting
and reselecting inside each leave-one-out fold (it is not a frozen
octane-trained model applied to decanes); Test~B pools all
$100$ molecules and adds the carbon number $n_C$ as a covariate, so
that each descriptor is judged on branching beyond size. Both use the
non-truncating design of Section~\ref{sec:ablation}: the parent is searched
over $(\alpha,\beta) \in (-12,12)^2$ and the Loyola model over the same
box with $\gamma \in (-2,2)$, with $2000$ candidates and seeds
$0$--$49$, and the pre-registered criterion for a $\gamma$ effect
required at least $40$ wins in $50$ seeds and a molecule-bootstrap
interval excluding zero (Table~\ref{tab:bp}).

either on the decane cohort (median $\Delta Q^2 = -0.072$, LO ahead
in $15$ of $50$ seeds, $95\%$ interval $[-0.156, +0.002]$) or in the
pooled size-adjusted set ($-0.000$, $25$ of $50$, $[-0.003, +0.002]$).
The pooled cohort was corrected after an external audit found that one
nonane's second NIST entry had been skipped (Deviation D5 in the
repository); on the uncorrected cohort of $101$ molecules the same test
gave $-0.000$, $24$ of $50$, $[-0.003, +0.002]$, so the correction
changes no conclusion.

data, not independent replications, and the intervals come from a
bootstrap over molecules conditional on the fitted predictions, so they
do not include the uncertainty of refitting and model selection. The baselines show where
the information is. Ridge regression on the ten degree-pair counts
reaches $Q^2 = 0.860$ on the decanes, where every single index stays
between $0.15$ and $0.51$, and $0.991$ on the pooled set; a permutation
check ($200$ random permutations of the boiling points) gives it a
median $Q^2$ of $-0.09$, so the gap is not an artefact of the
cross-validation procedure itself, although a permutation test cannot
exclude every possible form of leakage. Branching effects on the
boiling point are thus spread over several degree-pair counts in a way
that no one-dimensional summary of the family, with or without
$\gamma$, recovers. These conclusions concern acyclic alkanes, i.e.\
closely related hydrocarbons without rings or heteroatoms; they are
the primary predictive evidence of this paper, with the
vaporization-enthalpy analyses of Sections~\ref{sec:ablation}
and~\ref{sec:external} as secondary diagnostics. Data, rule,
exclusions and scripts are in the
repository.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

The Loyola family extends the Gutman--Milovanovi\'c family
$M_{\alpha,\beta}$~\cite{GutmanMilovanovic2020AKCE,
MolinaSigarreta2025GM} by an exponential imbalance factor and reduces to
it at $\gamma = 0$. Its structural content is exact: the imbalance direction
is affinely independent of the two parent directions for
$\Delta \ge 3$ (a class-level statement, Remark~\ref{rem:ident});
$\log LO$ is globally convex; the pure-$\gamma$ line discriminates
exactly the histogram of edge imbalances and loses degree-pair
information from tree order $13$ (order $16$ for $\Delta \le 4$); two
molecular graphs exchange order at most five times along the $\gamma$
axis; and the bounds of Section~\ref{sec:bounds} extend parent
inequalities to $\gamma \neq 0$. Generic discrimination is inherited
from the parent, and the imbalance coordinate re-weights degree-pair
information without adding to it.

Chemometrically, the evidence is diagnostic: every vertex-degree-based
index on the octanes operates above an irreducible floor set by two
degree-pair collision pairs, and the apparent vaporization-enthalpy
gain from freeing $\gamma$ --- seen in every seed of the octane sweep
and meeting the pre-registered criterion on the $34$ nonanes with values
(mostly compilation values) --- disappears once the parent family is searched
over a box that does not truncate it (Table~\ref{tab:box}). Tuning
$\gamma$ thus yields no demonstrated predictive advantage under the
protocols used here, which does not exclude benefits on other
properties, compound classes or search designs; the untuned point $LO(0,0,1)$, essentially $IRLA$, matches the
tuned models, and ridge regression on all degree-pair counts predicts
the nonanes better than every index model. Pre-registered tests on
measured boiling points complement this on larger data: no demonstrated
benefit from
$\gamma$ on the $34$ decanes or the $100$ pooled C6--C10 alkanes
adjusted for size, with ridge on the degree-pair counts far ahead of
every single index (Table~\ref{tab:bp}). The compactness of
$LO(0,0,1)$ belongs to linear imbalance weighting and is shared by the
parameter-free $IRLA$; what the third coordinate itself offers is an
interpretable, analytically tractable direction and an exact account
of what the imbalance direction can and cannot resolve; open problems are
listed in Supplementary Section~S8.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Data, scripts and verifiers that recompute
every tabulated value are at
\url{https://github.com/Singati2/LOYOLA-PAPER}; the nonane and
boiling-point protocols were committed there before the corresponding
models were fitted, and a separate script reproduces the structural
counts (tree orders up to $17$, the crossing and $GA$ witnesses, the
identifiability check) and the auxiliary values quoted in the text. The inequalities of
Propositions~\ref{thm:upper_Delta} and~\ref{thm:lower_delta}, of
Corollary~\ref{thm:cs} and Proposition~\ref{thm:ratio_bound}, the
convexity part of Theorem~\ref{thm:logconvex}, the rank-$4$ statement
of Lemma~\ref{prop:indep} for $\Delta \ge 3$, and the order-$13$
profile collision of Section~\ref{sec:degeneracy} have additionally been
machine-checked in Lean~4 with Mathlib (graphs modelled by their
multisets of edge degree pairs; equality characterizations not
formalized); its scope and encoding are described in Supplementary
Section~S7, and the formalization is in the repository.
