### What the decane result supports, and what it does not

**Supported:**
1. The registered criterion for a γ effect fails on both counts. LO$_h$ leads in only 15 of 50 seeds against the required 40, and the lower end of the 95% interval, −0.156, is below 0.
2. The point estimate favours the parent: median paired ΔQ² = −0.072. LO$_h$ is behind in 35/50 seeds, and by more than 0.1 in 22 of them (own recomputation).
3. The interval only just reaches zero (upper end +0.0015, displayed +0.001). Under the registered, conditional interval, the data come close to showing a loss from γ, not a gain.

**Not supported:**
1. **A loss from γ.** The interval includes 0. It is also conditional on the fitted predictions and omits refitting and selection (M 1360, 1399–1400), so it is anti-conservative. The selection-aware resampling range is [−0.510, +0.312] on 27-molecule subsamples (M 1392–1393).
2. **Equivalence or "no effect".** No margin was registered (M 1401–1402).
3. **Anything about the ΔH$_{vap}$ gain.** The test uses $T_B$, a property on which γ had given mixed results (M 1323–1325).
4. **Generalisation beyond the 34 NIST-covered decanes.** Representativeness is not shown (M 1332–1334). The result also does not extend to other carbon numbers or to cyclic compounds.
5. **A fair 2-D versus 3-D comparison.** The budgets are equal, but LO$_h$ searches three dimensions (M 1402–1404).

**Context:** both tuned single-index models are weak on the decanes (Q² 0.41–0.51, against ridge 0.86). The contrast is therefore between two models that each leave most within-carbon-number variance unexplained.

**Assessment:** the text at M 1375–1381 and 1398–1407 states this correctly. No change is required. See OPTIONAL row M-T5d for a sentence the authors could add.
