## 1. Verdict

**Choose (A): the corrected long manuscript, tightened in place in `main.tex`.**

* **Edits.** Move about 8–9 pages of routine or duplicated material into the supplement. Most of it goes to a new Section S10; the rest goes to S6 and S9. Keep the section order and the number of Theorem 2. Port the two caveat improvements that only the short draft has.
* **Expected length.** About 35–36 A5 pages plus a supplement of about 33 pages.
* **The short draft.** Archive `drafts/short/` as a record of the reframing and do not maintain it as a parallel candidate. Do not make a third "hybrid" file. Every tightening move the short draft demonstrates can be applied to `main.tex` directly.

**Why not (B), the short draft.**

* **Not verified.** It is the version the repository cannot verify (Section 2.3).
* **Stale.** It predates the Figure 1 move, and regenerating it now produces a dangling reference.
* **Factual mismatch.** It contains at least one claim that nothing in the draft supports: "both cohorts reported" in the deviations table.
* **Lost caveats.** It dropped several qualifications that the long version carries (Section 2.4).
* **Open approval.** It needs coauthor approval of a new title and framing (checklist item B1, still open).
* **Cost.** Bringing it to the long version's verification standard means re-keying the prose registries, `check_labels.py`, `LEAN_VERIFICATION.md` and the supplement cross-references (checklist N5). That is more work and more risk than tightening `main.tex`, whose 758 registered prose numbers pass today.

**Why not a hybrid file.** The only things the short draft does better are these:

* moves (structure sensitivity to S6; closed forms and termwise bounds to the supplement; cutting the limits proposition);
* two caveats (the octane ridge result; the LO-minus-ridge interval on the nonanes);
* the reader-facing framing of the abstract and introduction.

All of them can be transplanted into `main.tex` without changing which file the tools check. A separate hybrid would add a third source of truth and is not shown to be better.

**One constraint drives the move decisions.** MATCH publishes only the paper (`audit/MATCH_FORMAT_AND_REFERENCE_CHECK.md`, row "Supplementary material"). The supplement exists only as `LOYOLA_supplement.pdf` in the repository. So only material that is **not a claimed contribution** may leave `main.tex`:

* Routine extensions (closed forms, termwise bounds): may move.
* Duplicates of supplement text (most of Section 4.3; half of Section 4.2): may move.
* Proofs of the theorems and propositions that the abstract, introduction or conclusion claims: stay.
* Every caveat, deviation and limitation: stays.

The novelty matrix (`audit/NOVELTY_AND_PROOF_MATRIX.md`, row 13) already classes the closed forms and the bracket, Cauchy–Schwarz, (Δ, δ) and ratio bounds as "routine extensions".
