**What the paper does.** The Loyola family multiplies each edge term of the Gutman–Milovanović index by
$e^{\gamma q_{uv}}$, where $q_{uv}$ is the normalised degree imbalance of the edge. Building on theorems of
Rada, the pure-imbalance points at algebraic $\gamma \ne 0$ separate exactly the histograms of edge
imbalances, and no degree-ratio index separates more. Our main new result is where this ceiling bites: by
exhaustive enumeration with exact (radical and transcendence-based) tie certification, the imbalance
histogram determines the degree-pair counts of a tree up to order 12 (15 for trees of maximum degree at
most 4), with explicit witnesses at orders 13 and 16 and at every larger order. We also bound the number of
order exchanges of two chemical graphs along $\gamma$ (at most five) and identify the path and the star as
extremal trees of the pure-imbalance points.

**Relation to earlier work.** The separation statement is Rada's theorem applied to this family, and the
linear dependence of every bond-incident-degree index on the edge-degree-pair counts is classical (Rada,
MATCH 96 (2026)); we credit both in the text. The new content is the exact minimal orders 13 and 16 with
their witnesses, the crossing bound and the extremal trees.

**Related work by the same authors.** The three authors are also preparing a separate manuscript, "Ten
Edge-Degree Counts Are All a Bond-Incident-Degree Index Can See: An Exact Redundancy Certificate and What It
Leaves for New Molecular Descriptors" (G. Shiwakoti, A. Natarajan, M. Arockiaraj). It is [not submitted to
any journal and not posted on a preprint server — authors to confirm, including for the June 2026

**Chemistry.** We test the family on octane properties, a pre-registered replication on nonanes and
pre-registered tests on measured boiling points of acyclic alkanes. The results are negative or modest and
are reported as such: the boiling-point tests did not demonstrate a predictive gain from $\gamma$, and a gain
on vaporisation enthalpies that met a pre-registered seed-count rule shrinks to about zero in an
exploratory comparison with a wider search box. Source limitations of the experimental values are stated
in the paper and the supplement.
