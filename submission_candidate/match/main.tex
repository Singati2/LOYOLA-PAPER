\begin{abstract}%\blfootnote{\doclicenseThis}
  Vertex-degree-based indices cannot distinguish graphs with equal
  edge-degree-pair counts, and indices that depend on each edge only
  through its degree ratio lose further information. We determine where
  this further loss begins for trees. The \emph{Loyola index family}
  $LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ multiplies the edge term
  $(d_u d_v)^{\alpha} (d_u + d_v)^{\beta}$ of the Gutman--Milovanovi\'c
  index by $e^{\gamma q_{uv}}$, where $q_{uv} = |d_u - d_v|/(d_u + d_v)$
  is the normalised degree imbalance of the edge. By theorems of Rada, the
  points $LO(G;0,0,\gamma)$ at algebraic $\gamma \neq 0$ separate exactly the
  histograms of edge imbalances, and no degree-ratio index, such as the
  geometric--arithmetic or degree-ratio Sombor index, separates more. By
  exhaustive enumeration, this histogram determines the degree-pair counts
  of every tree up to order twelve and of every chemical tree, the carbon
  skeleton of an alkane, up to order fifteen. Explicit counterexamples
  exist at every larger order. Two chemical graphs exchange order at most
  five times along $\gamma$. Pre-registered tests on measured alkane
  boiling points did not demonstrate a predictive gain from $\gamma$. A
  vaporisation-enthalpy gain that met a pre-registered seed-count rule
  shrank to about zero in an exploratory rerun with a search box that did
  not truncate the parent index.
\end{abstract}

\onehalfspacing

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
  % =====================================================================

  Every vertex-degree-based index depends on a graph only through its
  edge-degree-pair counts, so graphs with equal counts are never
  separated~\cite{Rada2026LinearVDB}. On graphs with $\Delta \le 4$ every BID index is a linear functional
  of the ten edge-degree-pair counts, a representation used
  in~\cite{Rada2019ExpVDB, RadaRodriguezSigarreta2022Weak} and developed
  systematically in~\cite{Rada2026LinearVDB}. An index can lose further
  information through the form of its edge weights. If the weight of an
  edge depends only on the ratio of its end degrees, the index sees only
  the histogram of these ratios. This paper asks at which tree orders that
  second loss begins, and what a parameter for the degree imbalance adds
  inside the space of counts.

  Degree-based topological indices form one of the most developed
  descriptor classes of chemical graph theory. The class runs from the
  Randi\'c and Zagreb indices~\cite{Randic1975, GutmanTrinajstic1972}
  and their parametric extensions, the general Randi\'c and
  sum-connectivity families~\cite{BollobasErdos1998, ZhouTrinajstic2010},
  to the Sombor index~\cite{Gutman2021Sombor} and its many variants, surveyed in~\cite{Gutman2026SomborSurvey}. Extremal results on
  bond-incident-degree (BID) indices are surveyed
  in~\cite{AliGutmanFurtula2026BID}, and a finite-dimensional linear
  framework for such indices is developed in~\cite{Rada2026LinearVDB}.

  The \emph{Gutman--Milovanovi\'c family}~\cite{GutmanMilovanovic2020AKCE},
  studied in the recent bounds and QSPR literature
  \cite{GranadosJMC2025GM, MolinaSigarreta2025GM, PortillaJMC2025GM},
  is $M_{\alpha,\beta}(G) = \sum_{uv \in E(G)}
  (d_u d_v)^{\alpha} (d_u + d_v)^{\beta}$, $\alpha, \beta \in
  \mathbb{R}$. Its special cases, catalogued
  in~\cite{MolinaSigarreta2025GM}, include $M_{0,1} = M_1$,
  $M_{1,0} = M_2$, the hyper-Zagreb index
  $M_{0,2} = HM$~\cite{ShirdelRezapourSayadi2013HM},
  $M_{-1,0} = {}^{m}\!M_2$, the Randi\'c index $M_{-1/2,0} = R$,
  the sum-connectivity index
  $M_{0,-1/2} = \chi$~\cite{ZhouTrinajstic2009sumconn}, the harmonic
  index $2M_{0,-1} = H$, the inverse sum indeg index $M_{1,-1} = ISI$,
  the geometric--arithmetic index
  $2M_{1/2,-1} = GA$~\cite{VukicevicFurtula2009GA} and the
  arithmetic--geometric index $\tfrac12 M_{-1/2,1} = AG$. Its two
  parameter axes are the general Randi\'c and sum-connectivity
  families, and parameter-optimised versions have already been fitted
  to octane properties~\cite{AliGutmanFurtulaAlbalahiHamza2025}.

  We study the three-parameter extension
  \[
  LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma) = \sum_{uv \in E(G)}
  (d_u d_v)^{\alpha}(d_u+d_v)^{\beta} e^{\gamma q_{uv}},
  \qquad q_{uv} = \frac{|d_u - d_v|}{d_u + d_v},
  \]
  the \emph{Loyola index family}, with $LO(G;\alpha,\beta,0) =
  M_{\alpha,\beta}(G)$. Positive $\gamma$ upweights degree-imbalanced
  edges, negative $\gamma$ downweights them, and on regular graphs
  $\gamma$ has no effect. We ask what $\gamma$ adds. It reads no graph information beyond the degree-pair counts, but at rational parent exponents it can separate graphs that the parent merges.

  The family is a three-parameter subfamily of the exponential
  vertex-degree-based indices of Rada~\cite{Rada2019ExpVDB}, whose
  extremal theory is developed
  in~\cite{CruzMonsalveRada2020, Sigarreta2022ExpVDB, GaoAMC2024} and, for general edge weights,
  in~\cite{HuMATCH2022}.
  Published exponential
  indices~\cite{DasMondal2024ExpGA, DasMondalRazaPal2025ExpISDD} exponentiate
  a single classical generator. The variable
  Euler--Sombor index~\cite{GutmanRedzepovicKizilirmakKulli2025EU} and
  the cosine-rule Sombor indices~\cite{NyauliBuragohain2026CoRSO} weight
  the imbalance polynomially and without normalisation. Functions of the ratio
  $q_{uv}$ appear in the diminished Sombor
  index~\cite{MovahediGutmanRedzepovicFurtula2026}, in degree-ratio
  indices~\cite{NyauliBuragohain2027DRSO, GutmanBuragohainNyauli2026},
  and, as a normalised form of Albertson's
  irregularity~\cite{Albertson1997, LinZhouWangMiao2022}, in
  $IRLA(G) = 2\sum_{uv} q_{uv}$~\cite{RetiSharafdini2018IRLA}. The
  pure-$\gamma$ point $LO(G;0,0,\gamma) = \sum_{uv} e^{\gamma q_{uv}}$
  is the exponential index, in the sense of~\cite{Rada2019ExpVDB}, of
  the edge function $\gamma q_{uv}$. The
  coordinate added here is linear in $q_{uv}$ in the exponent and is
  coupled with its own parameter to the Gutman--Milovanovi\'c family. It makes
  every edge weight log-linear in $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$, so $LO$ is
  the partition function of an exponential family on the edges of $G$.

  For $\Delta \le 4$ the whole family therefore lies in the fixed
  ten-dimensional space of linear functionals of the counts. Our questions
  concern a nonlinear family inside that space: which count vectors a
  parameter point separates, and how the index moves along $\gamma$.
  The tools are classical. Gutman's survey~\cite{Gutman2026SomborSurvey}
  observes that new variants hardly ever improve on earlier ones in
  applications, and our tests agree. The family is not proposed as a
  better predictor, and the QSPR sets used here are small.

  What is new here is where the known results bind for chemical graphs. By
  exhaustive enumeration, the $q$-histogram carries the full
  degree-pair information of an alkane carbon skeleton up to order $15$
  and not beyond, so no degree-ratio index, such as $GA$, $AG$ or the
  diminished Sombor index, separates all skeletons of order $16$ or more.
  Along $\gamma$ the order of two chemical graphs changes at most five
  times.

  The results below differ in status, and we group them accordingly.
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] \emph{Known theorems applied to the family.} That the
  pure-$\gamma$ points at algebraic $\gamma \neq 0$ separate exactly the
  $q$-histograms is Theorem~3.4 of~\cite{Rada2019ExpVDB} applied to the
  weights $\gamma q_{ij}$, and that no degree-ratio index separates more
  is Proposition~2.5 there (Theorem~\ref{thm:ratioplane}(ii) and the corollary on degree-ratio
  indices in Section~\ref{sec:ratioplane}). The same Lindemann--Weierstrass
  argument, with algebraic coefficients, shows that at rational exponents
  a nonzero algebraic $\gamma$ never merges two graphs that the parent
  separates (Proposition~\ref{prop:offplane}). The extremal trees of the
  pure-$\gamma$ points follow from the criteria of Cruz and
  Rada~\cite{CruzRada2019PathStar} (Section~\ref{sec:extremal}). Two
  octane collision pairs realise the known degree-pair
  ceiling~\cite[Section~3.1]{Rada2026LinearVDB}
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:collision}).
  \item[(ii)] \emph{Results proved here.} Explicit trees with distinct
  profiles and equal $q$-histograms at every order from $13$, and
  from $16$ for $\Delta \le 4$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:ratioplane}(iii)), carried
  by the corollary to every degree-ratio index. A bound on the number of
  order exchanges along $\gamma$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:laguerre}) and
  per-graph identifiability (Supplementary Section~S10). Two elementary facts: the imbalance
  direction is affinely independent of the parent coordinates once a
  vertex of degree at least $3$ is admitted (Lemma~\ref{prop:indep}), and
  $\log LO$ is a log-sum-exp and hence convex
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:logconvex}).
  \item[(iii)] \emph{Computer-assisted results.} That these orders are
  the first is established by exhaustive enumeration, as are the value
  counts for trees of orders $7$ to $17$ (Section~\ref{sec:discrimination}).
  \item[(iv)] \emph{A diagnostic application.} Structure sensitivity on
  the decanes (Section~\ref{sec:ss}), a matched-budget QSPR diagnostic and
  pre-registered tests on measured boiling points
  (Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}). Freeing $\gamma$ gives no demonstrated
  gain once the parent's search box does not truncate it
  (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). On measured boiling points ridge
  regression on the degree-pair counts leads every single index evaluated
  in point estimate (Section~\ref{sec:bp}).
  \end{enumerate}

\section{Preliminaries and notation}\label{sec:prelim}\label{sec:notation}\label{sec:def}
% =====================================================================

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a finite simple connected graph with at least one edge, of order
$n = |V|$ and size $m = |E|$, and for a vertex $v \in V$ let the degree
$d_v = d_G(v)$ count the edges incident to $v$. The maximum and
minimum degrees are denoted $\Delta = \Delta(G)$ and
$\delta = \delta(G)$. The chemometric sections use hydrogen-suppressed
molecular graphs of alkane carbon skeletons, for which $\Delta \le 4$,
while the mathematical results do not assume this bound unless stated.

For an edge $e = uv \in E(G)$ we write $(d_u, d_v)$ for its
\emph{degree pair}, understood as an unordered multiset
$\{d_u, d_v\}$. For integers $1 \le i \le j$ let
$m_{ij}(G) = |\{uv \in E(G) : \{d_u, d_v\} = \{i, j\}\}|$ be the number
of edges with degree pair $(i,j)$, called $(i,j)$-edges; the vector
$(m_{ij}(G))_{i \le j}$ is the \emph{degree-pair profile} (or count
vector) of $G$.

\begin{definition}[Loyola index family]\label{def:wuzi}
Let $G = (V, E)$ be a simple connected graph. For
$\alpha, \beta, \gamma \in \mathbb{R}$, the \emph{Loyola index family}
is defined as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wuzi}
\Wuzi
\;=\; \sum_{uv \in E(G)}
(d_u d_v)^{\alpha}\,(d_u + d_v)^{\beta}\,
\exp\!\left(\gamma\,\frac{|d_u - d_v|}{d_u + d_v}\right)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Eq.~\eqref{eq:wuzi} is defined for all $\alpha, \beta, \gamma \in \mathbb{R}$
on graphs without isolated vertices. Write
$q_{uv} = \mathrm{imb}(d_u, d_v)$, where $\mathrm{imb}(x,y) = |x - y|/(x + y)$,
for the normalised degree imbalance of the edge $uv$, and $q_{ij} = (j - i)/(i + j)$ for $i \le j$. Each
summand depends only on the unordered degree pair of its edge, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairsum}
LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma) = \sum_{i \le j} m_{ij}(G)\,
(ij)^{\alpha}(i+j)^{\beta} e^{\gamma q_{ij}} .
\end{equation}
In particular $LO(G;\alpha,\beta,0) = M_{\alpha,\beta}(G)$, the
Gutman--Milovanovi\'c family~\cite{GutmanMilovanovic2020AKCE, MolinaSigarreta2025GM},
and $LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma) = M_{\alpha,\beta}(G)$ for every
$\gamma$ when $G$ is regular.

\begin{definition}[$q$-histogram]\label{def:qhist}
The $q$-histogram of $G$ is the function
$n_q(G) = \sum_{i \le j,\; q_{ij} = q} m_{ij}(G)$, $q \in [0,1)$,
the number of edges of $G$ with imbalance $q$.
\end{definition}

Equal profiles give equal $q$-histograms. The converse fails
(Theorem~\ref{thm:ratioplane}(iii)).
 % =====================================================================

  \section{Parameter geometry}\label{sec:geometry}

  Throughout this section $\theta = (\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ and
  $z_{uv} = \bigl(\ln(d_u d_v),\, \ln(d_u + d_v),\, q_{uv}\bigr)$, so that
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:expfamily}
  LO_G(\theta) = \sum_{uv \in E(G)} e^{\theta^{\top} z_{uv}} .
  \end{equation}
  For a degree pair $(i,j)$ we write $z_{ij}$, and call
  $(1, z_{ij}) \in \mathbb{R}^4$ its augmented feature vector. A set of
  augmented vectors spans $\mathbb{R}^4$ if and only if the
  corresponding $z_{ij}$ do not lie in one affine plane of
  $\mathbb{R}^3$.

  \begin{lemma}[Affine independence of the imbalance direction]\label{prop:indep}
  For $\Delta \ge 3$, with
  $P_{\Delta} = \{(i,j) : 1 \le i \le j \le \Delta\}$ the
  admissible degree pairs, the augmented feature vectors
  $\bigl(1, \ln(ij), \ln(i+j), q_{ij}\bigr)$, $(i,j) \in P_{\Delta}$,
  span $\mathbb{R}^4$. Consequently, if two parameter triples
  $\theta, \theta'$ satisfy
  $e^{\theta^{\top} z} = c\, e^{\theta'^{\top} z}$ for some constant
  $c > 0$ and all $(i,j) \in P_{\Delta}$, then $\theta = \theta'$ and
  $c = 1$. In particular, no choice of $(\alpha', \beta')$ and scaling
  constant reproduces the action of $\gamma \neq 0$ on all admissible
  degree pairs. For $\Delta = 2$ the span has dimension $3$, while for
  $\Delta = 1$ the only pair is $(1,1)$, with augmented vector
  $(1, 0, \ln 2, 0)$, and the span has dimension $1$. In both cases the conclusion fails. For $\Delta = 2$, indeed, $q_{ij} = [\ln(i+j) - \ln 2 - \tfrac12 \ln(ij)]/(3\ln 3 - \tfrac92 \ln 2)$ on the three pairs, so for $\Delta = 2$ the factor $e^{\gamma q_{ij}}$ equals a constant times $(ij)^{a}(i+j)^{b}$ on the three pairs, for suitable constants $a, b$. The effect of $\gamma$ is therefore reproduced exactly by a shift of $(\alpha,\beta)$ together with a scaling constant.
  \end{lemma}

  The proof reduces the rank statement to $3^{8} \neq 2^{13}$ and is given
  in Supplementary Section~S10.

  By~\eqref{eq:expfamily}, $\log LO_G$ is a log-sum-exp function of
  $\theta$.

  \begin{theorem}[Log-convexity]\label{thm:logconvex}
  Fix a graph $G$ with $m \ge 1$ edges, define
  $F_G(\theta) = \log LO_G(\theta)$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$, and let
  $\mathbb{E}_{\theta}$ and $\operatorname{Cov}_{\theta}$ denote mean
  and covariance under the edge probability measure
  $w_{uv}(\theta) = e^{\theta^{\top} z_{uv}} / LO_G(\theta)$. Then
  $F_G$ is convex, with
  \begin{equation*}
  \nabla F_G(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\theta}[z], \qquad
  \nabla^2 F_G(\theta) = \operatorname{Cov}_{\theta}(z) \succeq 0 .
  \end{equation*}
  In particular
  $\partial_{\gamma} F_G = \mathbb{E}_{\theta}[q] \in [0, 1)$ and
  $\partial^2_{\gamma} F_G = \operatorname{Var}_{\theta}(q) \ge 0$, so
  $\log LO$ is non-decreasing and convex in $\gamma$, and it is strictly
  increasing in $\gamma$ unless every edge of $G$ joins vertices of
  equal degree. Moreover
  $\operatorname{Var}_{\theta}(q) = 0$ at some (equivalently, every)
  $\theta$ if and only if $q_{uv}$ is constant over the edges of $G$,
  which covers regular graphs ($q \equiv 0$) and, for example,
  $(\Delta,\delta)$-semiregular bipartite graphs with $\Delta > \delta$. A bipartite graph is
  $(\Delta,\delta)$-semiregular when every vertex on one side has
  degree $\Delta$ and every vertex on the other side has degree
  $\delta$, so every edge has degree pair $(\delta,\Delta)$. On these every edge carries the
  same nonzero imbalance, and $\log LO$ is affine and strictly
  increasing in $\gamma$. $F_G$ is strictly convex if and only if the
  edge feature vectors $\{z_{uv}\}_{uv \in E}$ do not lie in a proper
  affine subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$. On a regular graph
  $F_G(\theta) = \log m + \theta^{\top} z_0$ is affine and
  $\operatorname{Cov}_{\theta}(z) = 0$.
  \end{theorem}

  The proof is the standard computation of the gradient and Hessian of a
  log-sum-exp function and is given in Supplementary Section~S10.


  \subsection{The ratio plane, crossings and identifiability}\label{sec:ratioplane}

  Since $4ij/(i+j)^2 = 1 - q_{ij}^2$, every admissible pair satisfies
  $\ln(ij) = 2\ln(i+j) + \ln(1 - q_{ij}^2) - \ln 4$, and therefore
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:factor}
  LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma) = 4^{-\alpha} \sum_{uv \in E(G)}
  (d_u + d_v)^{2\alpha+\beta}\,(1 - q_{uv}^2)^{\alpha}\, e^{\gamma q_{uv}}.
  \end{equation}
  With $\kappa = 2\alpha + \beta$, the parameter $\kappa$ acts only on
  the degree sum $d_u + d_v$, and $\alpha$, $\gamma$ act only on
  $q_{uv}$, a function of the degree ratio $d_u/d_v$.

  \begin{definition}[Ratio plane]\label{def:ratioplane}
  The ratio plane is
  $\Pi = \{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma) \in \mathbb{R}^3 : \beta = -2\alpha\}$,
  the plane $\kappa = 0$. It contains the pure-$\gamma$ points
  $(0,0,\gamma)$, $GA = 2\,LO(\tfrac12,-1,0)$ and
  $AG = \tfrac12 LO(-\tfrac12,1,0)$.
  \end{definition}

  Graphs with equal profiles receive equal values of every
  vertex-degree-based index~\cite{Rada2019ExpVDB, Rada2026LinearVDB}, and
  sufficient linear criteria for an index to separate all distinct
  profiles within a class of graphs of fixed order are given
  in~\cite{RadaRodriguezSigarreta2022Weak, Rada2026LinearVDB}. The theorem below concerns a
  coarser loss. On $\Pi$ distinct profiles with equal $q$-histograms
  receive equal values. Part~(iii) shows that such profiles occur among
  trees of every order from $13$ (from $16$ when $\Delta \le 4$); that
  no smaller tree has them is a computer-assisted result (see the
  remark on the floor on $\Pi$ later in this subsection).

  \begin{theorem}[Ratio plane]\label{thm:ratioplane}
  Let $G$ and $H$ be graphs.
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] On $\Pi$,
  $LO(G;\alpha,-2\alpha,\gamma) = \sum_q n_q(G)\,\bigl((1-q^2)/4\bigr)^{\alpha}
  e^{\gamma q}$ depends on $G$ only through its $q$-histogram, and $G$ and
  $H$ agree at every point of $\Pi$ if and only if $n_q(G) = n_q(H)$
  for all $q$. Otherwise their values differ at every point of $\Pi$
  outside a set of measure zero.
  \item[(ii)] For every nonzero algebraic $\gamma$,
  $LO(G;0,0,\gamma) = LO(H;0,0,\gamma)$ if and only if
  $n_q(G) = n_q(H)$ for all $q$. On any finite set of graphs the same
  holds for all but finitely many real $\gamma$.
  \item[(iii)] Let $G$ and $H$ be trees with distinct
  edge-degree-pair profiles and equal $q$-histograms, each having a
  leaf adjacent to a vertex of the same degree $d \ge 2$. Attaching a pendant path of length $L \ge 1$ at such a leaf in each
  produces trees with the same two properties and the same maximum
  degree. The profiles change by the same vector, whether or not the
  chosen leaf edge was the only $(1,d)$-edge.
  Consequently, for every $n \ge 13$ (and every $n \ge 16$ among trees
  with $\Delta \le 4$) there are non-isomorphic trees of order $n$
  with distinct degree-pair profiles that no point of $\Pi$ separates.
  \end{enumerate}
  \end{theorem}

  \begin{proof}
  (i) The formula is immediate from~\eqref{eq:factor}, since setting
  $\beta = -2\alpha$ removes the degree-sum factor, and it shows that
  equal histograms give agreement at every point of $\Pi$. For the
  measure-zero clause, suppose the
  histograms differ. At $\alpha = 0$ the difference is
  $\sum_q (n_q(G) - n_q(H))\,e^{\gamma q}$, an exponential sum with
  distinct exponents and not all coefficients zero, so it is not
  identically zero. This uses the first of two standard facts: a nonzero
  exponential sum with distinct real exponents is not identically zero,
  because as $\gamma \to +\infty$ the term with the largest exponent
  dominates. The difference on $\Pi$ is therefore a nonzero
  real-analytic function of $(\alpha,\gamma)$, whose zero set has
  measure zero. This is the second fact: the zero set of a nonzero real-analytic function on $\mathbb{R}^2$ has Lebesgue measure zero~\cite{Mityagin2020}.
  (ii) Agreement for equal histograms is immediate from the formula in
  (i). Since degrees are integers, every imbalance $q$ is rational, so
  for algebraic $\gamma \neq 0$ the exponents
  $\gamma q$, $q \in \mathbb{Q}$, are distinct algebraic numbers. The
  Lindemann--Weierstrass theorem~\cite{Baker1975} says that for distinct
  algebraic numbers $x_1, \dots, x_k$ the exponentials
  $e^{x_1}, \dots, e^{x_k}$ are linearly independent over the field of
  algebraic numbers. Hence the numbers
  $e^{\gamma q}$ are linearly independent over the algebraic numbers,
  and the integer coefficients $n_q(G) - n_q(H)$ must all vanish. For real
  $\gamma$, a nonzero exponential sum with $k$ distinct real exponents
  has at most $k - 1$ real zeros (Proposition~\ref{prop:laguerre} below), and
  a finite set of graphs gives finitely many such sums. (iii) The last
  clause follows from (i) once the trees exist, since equal histograms
  give agreement on all of $\Pi$. The attachment, a device also used by Rada~\cite{Rada2026LinearVDB} for edge-type vectors, changes the profile of each tree by the same vector,
  $-(1,d) + (2,d) + (L-1)(2,2) + (1,2)$, so distinct profiles stay
  distinct and equal histograms stay equal. The new or changed
  vertices have degree at most $2$, so the maximum degree is unchanged
  when it is at least $2$. Trees realising the two order-$13$ profiles
  listed in Section~\ref{sec:degeneracy} are the following. Their edge
  lists confirm the common $q$-histogram displayed there: one edge of
  imbalance $0$, two of $\tfrac15$, one of $\tfrac13$, three of
  $\tfrac12$ and five of $\tfrac57$. $T_1$: a path
  $c\,a\,b\,d\,e\,\ell$ in which $c$ carries five pendant leaves, $b$ and
  $d$ carry one pendant leaf each and $\ell$ is a leaf, so that
  $d_c = 6$, $d_a = d_e = 2$, $d_b = d_d = 3$; its edges are
  $(1,6)^{5}, (2,6), (2,3)^{2}, (3,3), (1,3)^{2}, (1,2)$. $T_2$: a path
  $c\,b\,x\,y\,z$ in which $c$ carries five pendant leaves, $b$ one and
  $z$ two, so that $d_c = 6$, $d_b = d_z = 3$, $d_x = d_y = 2$; its
  edges are $(1,6)^{5}, (3,6), (1,3)^{3}, (2,3)^{2}, (2,2)$. Both have a
  leaf adjacent to a vertex of degree $d = 3$. Consider now the order-$16$ pair with $\Delta \le 4$. The profile
  $\{(1,2)^{2}, (1,4)^{8}, (2,4)^{2}, (4,4)^{3}\}$ is realised by a path
  $A\,B\,C\,D$ of vertices of degree $4$. Here $A$ and $D$ carry three
  pendant leaves each, while $B$ and $C$ carry one pendant leaf and one
  pendant path of length $2$ each. The profile $\{(1,4)^{8}, (2,2)^{3}, (2,4)^{4}\}$ is realised by
  three vertices $A, B, C$ of degree $4$. They are joined as $A\,w\,B$
  through a vertex $w$ of degree $2$ and as $B\,v_1 v_2 v_3 v_4\,C$
  through a path of four
  vertices of degree $2$. The vertices $A$, $B$ and $C$ carry three, two
  and three pendant leaves. Both trees have the
  $q$-histogram $\{0^{3}, \tfrac13^{4}, \tfrac35^{8}\}$, since the pairs
  $(1,2)$ and $(2,4)$ have $q = \tfrac13$, the pair $(1,4)$ has
  $q = \tfrac35$ and the pairs $(2,2)$ and $(4,4)$ have $q = 0$. Both also
  have a leaf adjacent to a vertex of degree $d = 4$, which gives every
  larger order.
  \end{proof}

  Part~(ii) is Theorem~3.4 of~\cite{Rada2019ExpVDB} applied to the algebraic edge weights $\gamma q_{ij}$, whose classes of equal weight are the $q$-classes: two graphs receive the same value for every algebraic $\gamma \neq 0$ exactly when they have equal $q$-histograms. The propagation by a pendant path in part~(iii) is the device of~\cite{Rada2026LinearVDB}. The construction parallels Example~2.2 of~\cite{RadaRodriguezSigarreta2022Weak}, where indices that depend only on the degree sum are defeated by chemical trees whose counts agree on classes of equal sum. Here the classes are those of equal ratio, which for $\Delta \le 4$ are $\{(1,2),(2,4)\}$ and $\{(2,2),(3,3),(4,4)\}$ (ignoring $(1,1)$, which occurs only in $K_2$).

  Song and Huang~\cite{SongHuang2023EDV} studied a class-wide question of the same kind for the edge division vector of a tree. It counts the edges $e$ of a tree $T$ by the order $\min\{n_u(e), n_v(e)\}$ of the smaller component of $T - e$ and so controls every edge-additive eccentric index, such as the Wiener index. They determine all such trees of order at most $10$. Neither partition of trees refines the other: the two seven-vertex trees with edge division vector $(4,1,1)$ have different degree-pair profiles and different $q$-histograms, while the two octane pairs of Table~\ref{tab:collisions} have different edge division vectors and different Wiener indices. Explicit trees with equal Randi\'c index and different class counts were given by Rada~\cite[Example~2.6]{Rada2019ExpVDB}, on $29$ vertices, and as an infinite family of chemical trees by Rada, Rodr\'{\i}guez and Sigarreta~\cite[Example~2.1]{RadaRodriguezSigarreta2022Weak}.

  We found no earlier determination of the orders $13$ and $16$, or of explicit colliding trees for degree-ratio indices, in a literature search recorded in the repository~\cite{LoyolaRepo}. The minimality of orders $13$ and $16$ is computer-assisted. It rests on an exhaustive enumeration of all trees of orders $2$ to $16$ in exact rational arithmetic, reproducible with the code of the repository~\cite{LoyolaRepo}.

  \begin{corollary}[Degree-ratio indices]\label{cor:ratioclass}
  Let $\rho_{uv} = \min(d_u,d_v)/\max(d_u,d_v)$ and let
  $I_f(G) = \sum_{uv \in E(G)} f(\rho_{uv})$ for any function
  $f$ on $(0,1]$. Since $\rho_{uv} = (1-q_{uv})/(1+q_{uv})$, $I_f$
  depends on $G$ only through its $q$-histogram. Consequently the trees of Theorem~\ref{thm:ratioplane}(iii), which
  exist at every order $n \ge 13$ (and $n \ge 16$ with $\Delta \le 4$),
  are not separated by any such index. This applies to $GA$, $AG$, the exponential geometric--arithmetic index~\cite{DasMondal2024ExpGA}, the diminished Sombor index~\cite{MovahediGutmanRedzepovicFurtula2026},
  the symmetric division deg index~\cite{VukicevicGasperov2010}, the
  inverse symmetric division deg index and its
  exponential~\cite{DasMondalRazaPal2025ExpISDD}, and the degree-ratio
  indices~\cite{NyauliBuragohain2027DRSO, GutmanBuragohainNyauli2026},
  including the degree-ratio Sombor index studied
  in~\cite{GutmanRedzepovicBuragohainNyauli2027, AliGutmanEtAl2027DRSO, ShaoJahanbani2027}.
  \end{corollary}

  \begin{remark}\label{rem:ratioclass}
  The corollary follows from the identity for $\rho_{uv}$; it is the structure observation of~\cite[Proposition~2.5]{Rada2019ExpVDB} for edge weights that depend only on the degree ratio, and what is new are the explicit witnesses and the orders at which they first occur. Within the
  class it describes, the pure-$\gamma$ points at algebraic
  $\gamma \neq 0$ attain the most possible: by part~(ii) they separate
  every pair of graphs with distinct $q$-histograms. $GA$ does not
  (see the remark on the floor on $\Pi$ below). The parent attains it as well: at
  $\gamma = 0$ part~(i) gives $\sum_q n_q\,((1-q^2)/4)^{\alpha}$, an
  exponential sum in $\alpha$ with distinct bases, which separates two
  distinct histograms at all but finitely many $\alpha$. The limit thus
  belongs to the dependence on the degree ratio, not to $\gamma$, which
  adds the form valid at every algebraic value. The orders $13$ and $16$ are the first at which the $q$-histogram, and
  hence every degree-ratio index simultaneously, fails to separate
  distinct profiles. A single index can fail earlier through coincidences
  among its values. The symmetric division deg index merges two distinct
  profiles of chemical trees already at order $9$, for instance
  $\{(1,2), (1,3)^{2}, (2,2)^{4}, (2,3)\}$ and
  $\{(1,2)^{3}, (1,3), (2,3)^{3}, (3,3)\}$, both with value $58/3$, and the
  inverse symmetric division deg index does so at order $10$ (exact
  enumeration in the repository).
  \end{remark}

  Off $\Pi$ the argument behind~\cite[Theorem~3.4]{Rada2019ExpVDB}
  applies unchanged when the coefficients are algebraic rather than
  integer. The following proposition records this direct consequence of
  the Lindemann--Weierstrass theorem, which is used in
  Section~\ref{sec:degeneracy}; we do not claim it as a new theorem.

  \begin{proposition}[Parent distinctions survive]\label{prop:offplane}
  Let $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{Q}$ and let $\gamma \ne 0$ be algebraic.
  For graphs $G$, $H$ and each imbalance value $q$ write
  $c_q(G,H) = \sum_{(i,j):\, q_{ij} = q} \bigl(m_{ij}(G) - m_{ij}(H)\bigr)
  (ij)^{\alpha} (i+j)^{\beta}$. Then
  $LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma) = LO(H;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ if and only if
  $c_q(G,H) = 0$ for every $q$. In particular, if
  $M_{\alpha,\beta}(G) \ne M_{\alpha,\beta}(H)$ then
  $LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma) \ne LO(H;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$: at rational
  exponents a nonzero algebraic $\gamma$ never destroys a distinction
  made by the parent.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
  The difference is $\sum_q c_q(G,H)\, e^{\gamma q}$, a sum over the
  distinct imbalances $q$ occurring in $G$ or $H$. For rational
  $\alpha, \beta$ the numbers $(ij)^{\alpha}(i+j)^{\beta}$ are
  algebraic, being rational powers of positive integers, so the
  coefficients $c_q(G,H)$, integer combinations of them, are algebraic. For
  algebraic $\gamma \ne 0$ the exponents $\gamma q$ are distinct algebraic
  numbers, so by the Lindemann--Weierstrass theorem~\cite{Baker1975}
  the sum vanishes only if every $c_q(G,H)$ vanishes. The last claim
  follows because $\sum_q c_q(G,H) = M_{\alpha,\beta}(G) - M_{\alpha,\beta}(H)$.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}\label{rem:offplane}
  At rational $\alpha$, $\beta$, two graphs merged by $M_{\alpha,\beta}$
  stay merged at an algebraic $\gamma \neq 0$ only if every class sum $c_q(G,H)$ vanishes, which is
  stronger than $M_{\alpha,\beta}(G) = M_{\alpha,\beta}(H)$. On the trees
  of order $16$, $\gamma = 1$ recovers the distinctions the parent loses
  at $(0,\tfrac12,0)$ and $(0,\tfrac13,0)$
  (Section~\ref{sec:degeneracy}). The algebraic hypothesis cannot be
  dropped: at $\gamma = \ln 4$,
  $LO(K_4;0,0,\gamma) = 6 = 3e^{\gamma/2} = LO(K_{1,3};0,0,\gamma)$.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[The floor on $\Pi$]\label{rem:floor}
  By exhaustive enumeration of all trees of orders $2$--$17$ in exact
  arithmetic (Table~\ref{tab:firstfail}), two trees
  of order at most $12$ with equal $q$-histograms have equal
  degree-pair profiles, and the same holds for trees with
  $\Delta \le 4$ of order at most $15$. With part~(iii), the
  pure-$\gamma$ points at algebraic $\gamma \neq 0$ attain the
  degree-pair floor of Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}, separation of all
  distinct profiles, exactly up to those orders and at no larger
  order. Among all trees, at orders $13$--$17$, generic points off $\Pi$
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:generic}) and the Randi\'c index take
  more distinct values on these trees than any point of $\Pi$. A point of $\Pi$ can also do
  worse. $GA$ merges two distinct $q$-histograms among trees first at
  order $17$, for instance
  $\{(1,5)^{10}, (2,2)^{2}, (5,5)^{2}, (2,5), (1,2)\}$ and
  $\{(4,5)^{3}, (1,4)^{5}, (1,5)^{6}, (2,5), (1,2)\}$, which have
  equal $GA$ and different $q$-histograms.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{proposition}[Order exchanges along $\gamma$]\label{prop:laguerre}
  Fix $(\alpha,\beta)$ and graphs $G$, $H$, let
  $q_1 < \dots < q_r$ be the distinct imbalances of the degree pairs
  occurring in $G$ or $H$, and write
  $D(\gamma) = LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma) - LO(H;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
  = \sum_{k=1}^{r} c_k e^{\gamma q_k}$ with
  $c_k = \sum_{q_{ij} = q_k} (m_{ij}(G) - m_{ij}(H))(ij)^{\alpha}(i+j)^{\beta}$.
  Let $V$ be the number of sign changes in $(c_1,\dots,c_r)$ after
  zero terms are removed. If $D \not\equiv 0$, then $D$ has at most
  $V \le r - 1$ real zeros counted with multiplicity, and their number
  has the parity of $V$. In particular, for graphs with $\Delta \le 4$
  the possible imbalances are $0, \tfrac17, \tfrac15, \tfrac13,
  \tfrac12, \tfrac35$, and two such graphs exchange order at most five times as
  $\gamma$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$, for every $(\alpha,\beta)$.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
  With $x = e^{\gamma} > 0$, $D$ becomes the generalised polynomial
  $\sum_k c_k x^{q_k}$ with distinct real exponents, so Laguerre's
  extension of Descartes' rule of signs applies~\cite{Jameson2006}.
  Consider a finite sum $\sum_k c_k x^{q_k}$ with real exponents
  $q_1 < \dots < q_r$ and let $V$ count the sign changes in its
  coefficient sequence. The rule says that the number of zeros in
  $x > 0$, counted with multiplicity, is at most $V$ and differs from
  $V$ by an even number. Zeros of
  $D$ in $\gamma$ correspond one-to-one, with multiplicity, to positive
  zeros in $x$. The
  number of real zeros of $D$, with multiplicity, is therefore at most $V$ and has
  its parity. As an independent check, the parity also follows from the signs of $D$
  as $\gamma \to \mp\infty$, which are the signs of the first and last nonzero $c_k$.
  Divide $D$ by $e^{\gamma q_k}$ for the first $k$ with $c_k \neq 0$ and
  let $\gamma \to -\infty$, or for the last such $k$ and let
  $\gamma \to +\infty$. Only $c_k$ survives.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[Sharpness]\label{rem:crossings}
  The bound is attained. The trees of order $12$ with profiles
  $\{(1,3)^{2},\allowbreak (1,4)^{2},\allowbreak (1,6)^{5},\allowbreak (3,4),\allowbreak (4,6)\}$
  and
  $\{(1,2),\allowbreak (1,5)^{4},\allowbreak (1,6)^{4},\allowbreak (2,6),\allowbreak (5,6)\}$
  have $\Delta = 6$, eight distinct imbalances and $V = 5$, and at
  $(\alpha,\beta) = (-1,0)$ they cross at $\gamma \approx -17.2$,
  $-12.0$, $-2.0$, $2.7$ and $31.1$, which are therefore all zeros of
  $D$. The first tree is a vertex of degree $6$ with five leaves and a
  neighbour of degree $4$, which carries two leaves and a vertex of
  degree $3$ with two leaves. The second is a vertex of degree $6$ with
  four leaves, a neighbour of degree $5$ with four leaves, and a
  neighbour of degree $2$ ending in a leaf. Whether five crossings are
  attained for $\Delta \le 4$ is open. Along a line
  $\theta + t v$ not parallel to the $\gamma$ axis the exponents of $D$
  are the values $v^{\top} z_{ij}$, of which there can be as many as
  admissible pairs, so the constant five is specific to the $\gamma$
  axis.
  \end{remark}

  Per-graph identifiability, the directions along which the index of a
  single graph merely rescales, and the limits of $LO$ along the
  $\gamma$ axis are treated in Supplementary Section~S10.

  \begin{remark}[Data sets]\label{rem:dataset}
  For a QSPR data set the relevant set is the union of the pairs
  realised by its molecules. A direction that rescales every
  molecule's index by the same factor leaves every correlation
  unchanged and is absorbed into a regression slope. On the octanes
  this union contains nine of the ten admissible pairs and its affine
  dimension is $3$, so the octane fits of
  Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity} are free of exact confounding of this
  kind.
  \end{remark}

\subsection{The degree-pair ceiling}\label{sec:ceiling}

No vertex-degree-based index separates graphs with equal degree-pair
counts, so the number of distinct profiles is a \emph{ceiling} on
distinct values and a \emph{floor} on degeneracy.

  \begin{proposition}[Degree-pair ceiling]\label{prop:collision}
  If two graphs $G$ and $H$ have identical unordered
  edge-degree-pair count vectors, $m_{ij}(G) = m_{ij}(H)$ for all
  $i \le j$, then $LO(G;\theta) = LO(H;\theta)$ for every
  $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and more generally
  $TI(G) = TI(H)$ for every vertex-degree-based index $TI$. Among the
  $18$ octane isomers exactly $16$ distinct count vectors occur, and the
  pairs $\{$3-methylheptane, 4-methylheptane$\}$ and
  $\{$3,4-dimethylhexane, 3-ethyl-2-methylpentane$\}$ share their
  count vectors but differ in measured properties
  (Table~\ref{tab:collisions}). Hence every vertex-degree-based
  descriptor, tuned or not, carries a nonzero irreducible in-sample error on this benchmark.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
  By Definition~\ref{def:wuzi}, $LO(G;\theta)$ depends on $G$ only
  through the multiset of unordered edge degree pairs, that is, through
  $(m_{ij}(G))_{i \le j}$, and the same holds for every vertex-degree-based
  index~\cite{Rada2026LinearVDB}, since these graphs share the M-polynomial~\cite{DeutschKlavzar2015}. The counts for the $18$ octane
  isomers are computed and cross-checked in the reproducibility
  package, and Table~\ref{tab:collisions} lists the common count
  vectors of both collision pairs.
  \end{proof}

  A function of the count vector predicts one value per collision
  class, so its sum of squared errors is at least the within-class sum
  of squares. For the octane boiling points this floor is
  $2.925\,^{\circ}$C$^2$, a root mean square of $0.403\,^{\circ}$C. It
  bounds a single fixed predictor, not the cross-validated errors of
  Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}, where each fold fits its own.

  \begin{remark}[Dimension of the counts]\label{rem:countdim}
  For a tree of order $n \ge 3$ with $\Delta \le 4$ the ten counts are
  not free. A $(1,1)$ edge would be a component $K_2$, so $m_{11} = 0$.
  There are $n - 1$ edges, and since a vertex of degree $d$ lies on $d$
  edges, $\sum_{i \le j} (1/i + 1/j)\,m_{ij} = n$ (see~\cite[eqs.~(3)--(4)]{CruzRada2019PathStar}). The nine remaining
  counts therefore have affine dimension at most $7$ at fixed $n$ and
  at most $8$ when $n$ varies. Both bounds are attained by the data
  sets of Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}: the centred count matrices of
  the $18$ octanes and the $34$ measured decanes have rank $7$, and that
  of the $100$ pooled alkanes has rank $8$.
  \end{remark}

  The ceiling is tight. Distinct count vectors are separated at almost
  every parameter choice.

  \begin{proposition}[Generic discrimination]\label{prop:generic}
  Fix $\Delta \ge 1$ and let $m \neq m'$ be two distinct
  edge-degree-pair count vectors supported on the admissible pairs
  $P_{\Delta}$. Then
  $D(\theta) = \sum_{(i,j) \in P_{\Delta}} (m_{ij} - m'_{ij})\,
  e^{\theta^{\top} z_{ij}}$ is a real-analytic function of $\theta$
  that is not identically zero, and its zero set has Lebesgue measure zero in $\mathbb{R}^3$~\cite{Mityagin2020}. Consequently, any two graphs with distinct
  count vectors are distinguished by $LO(\cdot\,;\theta)$ for almost
  every $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^3$, while only graphs with identical count
  vectors (Proposition~\ref{prop:collision}) are indistinguishable
  for all $\theta$.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
  The map $(i,j) \mapsto z_{ij}$ is injective on $P_{\Delta}$, since
  $\ln(ij)$ and $\ln(i+j)$ recover the product and the sum, which
  determine the unordered pair. Choose $v \in \mathbb{R}^3$ such that
  the inner products $v^{\top} z_{ij}$ are pairwise distinct, which
  excludes only finitely many hyperplanes of $v$. Along the ray
  $\theta = t v$, $D(tv) = \sum_k c_k e^{t \lambda_k}$ with distinct
  exponents $\lambda_k$ and coefficients
  $c_k = m_{i_kj_k} - m'_{i_kj_k}$ that are not all zero, and such an
  exponential sum vanishes identically only if all $c_k = 0$. Hence $D \not\equiv 0$
  on the ray, and so on $\mathbb{R}^3$. Since $D$ is a finite sum of
  exponentials of linear functionals, it is real-analytic on
  $\mathbb{R}^3$, and the zero set of a nonzero real-analytic
  function has Lebesgue measure zero.
  \end{proof}

  In the terminology of Rada, Rodr\'{\i}guez and Sigarreta~\cite{RadaRodriguezSigarreta2022Weak}, an index is weakly discriminating over a class when equal values force equal count vectors. They showed that weights linearly independent over $\mathbb{Z}$, for instance exponentials of distinct algebraic numbers, give weakly discriminating indices~\cite[Theorems~3.1 and~3.8]{RadaRodriguezSigarreta2022Weak}, and support restrictions of this criterion are given in~\cite[Theorem~3, Corollary~2]{Rada2026LinearVDB}. As there are countably many pairs of count vectors, Proposition~\ref{prop:generic} gives that almost every point of the family is weakly discriminating over every class at once. The proof uses only the injectivity of $(i,j) \mapsto (\ln(ij), \ln(i+j))$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:generic} is inherited from the parent $M_{\alpha,\beta}$ rather than conferred by $\gamma$. The parameter $\gamma$ adds no graph information beyond the counts, of which every vertex-degree-based index is a functional~\cite{Rada2026LinearVDB}, only the different weighting of Lemma~\ref{prop:indep}.

  At the rational exponents used in practice the parent can fail: the sum-connectivity index $\chi = M_{0,-1/2}$ and the index $M_{0,1/2}$ are not weakly discriminating over chemical trees~\cite[Example~2.2]{RadaRodriguezSigarreta2022Weak}. The imbalance coordinate separates the pair of trees used there for $\chi$.

  \begin{example}\label{ex:sumconn}
  For $t \ge 1$ the chemical trees $S$ and $T$ of order $17 + t$ in
  Example~2.2 of~\cite{RadaRodriguezSigarreta2022Weak} have
  $m(S) - m(T) = 6(1,3) + 4(2,3) + 4(3,3) - 4(1,4) - 6(2,2) - 4(2,4)$ and
  equal sum-connectivity index. At $(\alpha,\beta) = (0,-\tfrac12)$,
  \[
  D(\gamma) = LO(S) - LO(T) = 3\bigl(e^{\gamma/2} - 1\bigr)
  - \tfrac{4}{\sqrt5}\bigl(e^{3\gamma/5} - e^{\gamma/5}\bigr)
  + \tfrac{4}{\sqrt6}\bigl(1 - e^{\gamma/3}\bigr),
  \]
  so $D(0) = 0$ and $D(1) \approx 0.2255$. The separation is small for
  small $\gamma$, for instance $D(10^{-6}) \approx 2.4 \times 10^{-7}$.
  \end{example}

\subsection{Degeneracy across tree orders}\label{sec:degeneracy}

Degeneracy~\cite{Konstantinova1996Degeneracy} measures the failure to
discriminate non-isomorphic graphs, and we quantify it with the
distinct-value convention $100(1 - k/N)$, where $k$ is the number of
distinct values among $N = 106$ trees (Supplementary Section~S5). It is
not the share of trees whose value another tree also takes, which is
larger: for $LO(0,0,1)$, $45$ of the $106$ trees ($42.5\%$). On
the $106$ trees of order $10$ the pure-$\gamma$ points $LO(0, 0, 1)$
and $LO(0, 0, 2)$ attain $25.5\%$ ($79$ distinct values), tied with
$R$, $GA$ and $AG$. The $106$ trees realise only $79$ distinct
edge-degree-pair count vectors, so $25.5\%$ is the floor for
\emph{any} degree-pair-based index, while $M_1$ ($83.0\%$) and
$M_2$ ($67.0\%$) are the most degenerate (Figure~\ref{fig:degeneracy}).
For orders $7$ through $12$, on all trees and on those with
$\Delta \le 4$ (Supplementary Table~S4), the pure-$\gamma$ points and
$R$ attain the floor at every order. The index $\chi$ leaves it from
order $10$, with $77$ values against $79$ profiles.
Theorem~\ref{thm:ratioplane} makes this exact. The pure-$\gamma$
points separate precisely the $q$-histograms, which coincide with the
degree-pair profiles up to order $12$ (order $15$ for $\Delta \le 4$)
and not beyond. At order $13$ the profiles
$\{(1,2)^{1}, (1,3)^{2}, (1,6)^{5}, (2,3)^{2}, (2,6)^{1}, (3,3)^{1}\}$
and $\{(1,3)^{3}, (1,6)^{5}, (2,2)^{1}, (2,3)^{2}, (3,6)^{1}\}$
(exponents are edge multiplicities) share the $q$-histogram
$\{0^{1}, \tfrac15^{2}, \tfrac13^{1}, \tfrac12^{3}, \tfrac57^{5}\}$.
Table~\ref{tab:firstfail} collects the first failures, and the index
$R$ still attains the floor at order $13$ ($570$ values) and leaves it
at order $14$, where the pure-$\gamma$ points give $1090$ values.

Off the ratio plane the imbalance factor can restore discrimination

and at fixed fractional exponents the sign of the change depends on the
property (Supplementary Section~S5).

\subsection{Structure sensitivity}\label{sec:ss}

variant due to Raki\'c and Furtula~\cite{RakicFurtula2019SS}, was
implemented with the published protocol on the $75$ decane isomer
trees. For a tree, the similar structures are those obtained by
relocating a single edge within the molecular class. $SS$ and $Abr$
are the class means of the mean and the maximum relative change of the
index over these neighbours. The implementation reproduces the
published decane-class values of Barman and Das~\cite{BarmanDas2026HSO},
with eleven of fourteen $SS$/$Abr$ values agreeing exactly at the four
displayed decimals. Each of the other three agrees within one unit in
the fourth. The protocol and this control are detailed in Supplementary
Section~S6.

has a structure sensitivity indistinguishable at three decimals from
that of the hyper-Zagreb index
($0.1257$ against $0.1256$). Its abruptness is markedly smaller ($0.274$
against $0.313$). The sensitivity-to-abruptness ratios of the

$IRLA$, which these points approximate to first order, has a higher ratio

\section{Extremal trees for the pure-imbalance points}\label{sec:bounds}\label{sec:extremal}
% =====================================================================

Closed forms on standard graphs and termwise bounds, with their equality
cases, are given in Supplementary Section~S10.

\begin{proposition}[Extremal trees of the pure-$\gamma$ points]\label{prop:extremal}
Let $n \ge 4$ and $\gamma > 0$. Among all trees of order $n$, the
path $P_n$ is the unique minimiser and the star $S_n$ the unique
maximiser of $LO(\cdot\,;0,0,\gamma)$, with
\[
(n-3) + 2e^{\gamma/3} \;\le\; LO(T;0,0,\gamma) \;\le\; (n-1)\,e^{\gamma(n-2)/n}.
\]
For $\gamma < 0$ the roles of $P_n$ and $S_n$ are exchanged.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\gamma > 0$. Every edge $uv$ of a tree of order $n$ has
$q_{uv} \le (n-2)/n$, with equality if and only if $\{d_u,d_v\} =
\{1,n-1\}$, so $LO(T;0,0,\gamma) \le (n-1)e^{\gamma(n-2)/n}$ with
equality if and only if every edge is a $(1,n-1)$-edge, that is,
$T = S_n$. For the lower bound, a tree with $k \ge 2$ leaves has at
least $k$ edges incident to a leaf, and each such edge has
$q \ge \tfrac13$ because the other end has degree at least $2$ and
$(d-1)/(d+1)$ is increasing in $d$. Hence
$LO(T;0,0,\gamma) \ge (n-1-k) + k e^{\gamma/3} \ge (n-3) + 2e^{\gamma/3}$,
since $e^{\gamma/3} > 1$. Equality in the second inequality forces
$k = 2$, so $T = P_n$, and $P_n$ attains the bound because its two
pendant edges are $(1,2)$-edges and its other edges are $(2,2)$-edges.
For $\gamma < 0$ every inequality reverses.
\end{proof}

The two bounds of Proposition~\ref{prop:extremal} also follow from the criteria of Cruz and Rada~\cite{CruzRada2019PathStar}; the direct proof above is included because it is short and also gives the uniqueness of the extremal trees. For the weights $\varphi_{ij} = e^{\gamma q_{ij}}$ and $E = e^{\gamma/3}$, their function $f(i,j) = \tfrac{ij}{i+j}(\varphi_{ij} + 2\varphi_{12} - 3\varphi_{22})$ equals $2(E - 1)$ at $(1,2)$ and $(2,2)$. For $\gamma > 0$ it is larger at every other pair: if $i, j \ge 2$ and $(i,j) \ne (2,2)$ then $\varphi_{ij} + 2E - 3 \ge 2(E-1) > 0$ and $ij/(i+j) > 1$, and for $(1,j)$, $j \ge 3$, one has $f \ge \tfrac34(e^{\gamma/2} + 2E - 3) > 2(E-1)$, since with $x = e^{\gamma/6} > 1$ this reads $3x^3 - 2x^2 - 1 = (x-1)(3x^2 + x + 1) > 0$. Corollary~2.2 of~\cite{CruzRada2019PathStar} then gives the lower bound $LO(T;0,0,\gamma) \ge LO(P_n;0,0,\gamma)$, and their Corollary~3.2 gives the upper bound $LO(T;0,0,\gamma) \le LO(S_n;0,0,\gamma)$, because $e^{\gamma q}$ is largest at the pair $(1,n-1)$. These corollaries state the bounds without equality cases. Uniqueness is given by the direct proof above, and also by the identities of their Theorems~2.1 and~3.1, since the inequalities just shown are strict away from the pairs $(1,2)$ and $(2,2)$, and $e^{\gamma q_{ij}} < e^{\gamma(n-2)/n}$ away from $(1,n-1)$. For $\gamma < 0$ every inequality reverses. Gao and Gao~\cite{GaoAMC2024} give general sufficient conditions for the path to be the unique minimal tree and the star the unique maximal tree of an exponential vertex-degree-based index, a class to which $LO(\cdot\,;0,0,\gamma) = \sum_{uv} e^{\gamma q_{uv}}$ belongs. The star half also follows from the sufficient condition of~\cite[Section~4.2]{Rada2026LinearVDB}; the path half does not follow from the corresponding condition there, because the $(1,2)$-weight $e^{\gamma/3}$ exceeds the $(2,3)$-weight $e^{\gamma/5}$. The proof uses only the bounds on $q$ and does not extend verbatim to general $(\alpha,\beta)$, where the ordering of trees depends on the
scale exponent $2\alpha + \beta$ as well as on $\gamma$. Among
chemical trees ($\Delta \le 4$) the termwise bound (Supplementary Section~S10) gives
$LO(T;0,0,\gamma) \le (n-1)e^{3\gamma/5}$ for $\gamma > 0$, with
equality only for $n = 5$. The path remains the unique minimiser for $\gamma > 0$ among chemical trees by Proposition~\ref{prop:extremal}, while the maximal chemical trees for $n > 5$ are left open. The orders $4$ to $12$ were also checked by
enumeration in the reproducibility package.

\section{QSPR diagnostics}\label{sec:sensitivity}
% =====================================================================

This section asks whether the family, and $\gamma$ in particular,
describes or predicts alkane properties better than the classical
indices it contains. The sets are small, so the octane
correlations are descriptive, and the pre-registered tests on measured
boiling points of Section~\ref{sec:bp} are the primary predictive
evidence.

We compare the family with the classical indices it reduces to on the
$18$ octane isomers, following the benchmark of the diminished Sombor
study~\cite{MovahediGutmanRedzepovicFurtula2026}, and use the $35$
nonane isomers as a replication set. The comparison set consists of
the exact reductions $M_1, M_2, HM, {}^{m}\!M_2, R, \chi, H/2, ISI, GA,
AG$ and the fixed pure-$\gamma$ points $LO(0, 0, 1)$ and $LO(0, 0, 2)$.
The edge count $m = LO(0, 0, 0)$ is constant on the octanes and is
omitted. No parameter in the correlation comparison is tuned against
the property data.

Predictive performance is measured by the leave-one-out coefficient
$Q^2 = 1 - \sum_i (y_i - \hat y_{(i)})^2 / \sum_i (y_i - \bar y)^2$,
where $\hat y_{(i)}$ predicts molecule $i$ from a model fitted without it
and $\bar y$ is the mean over all molecules. For a fixed descriptor, such
as $LO(0,0,1)$ or $IRLA$, ordinary leave-one-out refits only the
regression in each fold. Where a parameter point, an index or a ridge
penalty is chosen from the data, nested leave-one-out repeats that
choice in each fold on the training molecules alone, so the held-out
molecule affects neither selection nor fit. An ordinary leave-one-out
$Q^2$ of a point chosen on the same data omits the selection step and is
therefore optimistic.

The repository history begins with the octane correlations already
computed, so it cannot document that the benchmark points $\gamma = 1$ and
$2$ were fixed before the octane data were seen. An exploratory local-tuning check (Supplementary
Section~S2) exceeded the best fixed point only by a $0.001$ in-sample $\Delta H_{\mathrm{vap}}$ margin and, under nested leave-one-out, on octane $T_B$ ($0.764$ against $0.745$), a small point-estimate difference without an interval; no superiority is claimed.

\subsection{Effect of freeing $\gamma$ and of the search
box}\label{sec:ablation}

We ask whether, at identical search budgets, freeing $\gamma$ improves
out-of-sample prediction over an optimised parent $M_{\alpha,\beta}$.
In each outer fold of a nested leave-one-out (Supplementary Section~S3),
triples $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ for the Loyola model and pairs
$(\alpha,\beta)$ with $\gamma = 0$ for the parent are drawn uniformly
from a search box at equal budget. The candidate with the smallest
inner leave-one-out error is refit and predicts the held-out isomer.

In the box $(-2,2)$ freeing $\gamma$ improved the nested $Q^2$ for
$\Delta H_{\mathrm{vap}}$ in $100/100$ candidate streams at budgets
$200$ and $500$, with median $\Delta Q^2 = +0.069$ and $+0.068$
(Supplementary Table~S3), and in $93/100$ and $96/100$ with
2,2,3,3-tetramethylbutane excluded. It usually degraded $\Delta H_f$ and
was mixed elsewhere.

For $\Delta H_{\mathrm{vap}}$, however, the parent
chose $(\alpha,\beta) = (1.021, -1.993)$, on the boundary of the box, in
$16$ of the $18$ folds. By $\ln(ij) = 2\ln(i+j) + \ln(1-q^2) - \ln 4$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:factor}) the parent reaches imbalance information through
$(1-q^2)^{\alpha}$ along $\beta \approx -2\alpha$, which needs large
exponents and is the direction the box truncates.

The box of half-width $12$, chosen after inspecting the parent's nested $Q^2$ on a dense grid as the smallest examined in which the parent's selections are interior, is used below for every property (details in Supplementary Section~S11).

The matched comparison was repeated, without pre-registration, with
$2000$ candidates per model, seeds $0$--$49$ and boxes of half-width $2$
and $12$ (Supplementary Table~S6). In the wide box the
vaporisation-enthalpy gain shrinks to about zero. A hybrid control
widens only $(\alpha,\beta)$, keeps $\gamma \in (-2,2)$ and is drawn
after the parent from the same candidate stream. It gives median
$\Delta Q^2 = +0.004$ on the octanes ($28$ of $50$ seeds) and $-0.002$
on the nonanes ($24$ of $50$), while widening all three coordinates
gives $-0.010$ and $-0.016$. Most of the change comes from the parent
improving in the wide box ($0.887 \to 0.942$ on the octanes), the rest
from the three-dimensional search being slightly less efficient there
($0.957 \to 0.946$ for the hybrid control). On the other properties no
advantage reaches the seed-count criterion in either box, and octane $S$ moves from $11$ to $38$ wins, which shows how strongly the win count depends on the search protocol.

Under these protocols tuning $\gamma$ gives no
demonstrated advantage. Equal budgets do not give equally thorough
searches in two and three dimensions, so the design may cost the
three-parameter model part of the effect sought.

\subsection{Replication on the nonane isomers}\label{sec:external}

Under a protocol committed to the reproducibility package (commit
\texttt{964b127}) before the nonane data were committed and before any
model was fitted to them, the nested
design was applied to the $35$ constitutional nonane isomers. Boiling
points were $35$ values from the NIST WebBook (NIST averages where
given, otherwise the most recent determination, a deviation recorded in
the protocol). Standard vaporisation enthalpies were $34$ values from
the NIST WebBook~\cite{NISTWebBook}, of which $29$ are compiled values: NIST lists $4$ under Labbauf et al.~\cite{LabaufGreenshieldsRossini1961} (printed as Labauf) and $25$ under Reid~\cite{Reid1972}, a review of the handbook~\cite{WilhoitZwolinski1971}. Agreement with
them therefore measures consistency with compiled values (Supplementary
Section~S4).

The pre-registered verdict is that the $\gamma$ effect
\emph{replicates} for $\Delta H_{\mathrm{vap}}$, with LO better than GM
in $95/100$ and $98/100$ seeds and median $\Delta Q^2 = +0.048$ and
$+0.053$, and is \emph{mixed} for $T_B$ ($58/100$ and $63/100$). \emph{Replicates}
is the protocol's name for its seed-count rule, which the protocol
itself describes as a summary of optimiser variance and not an
inferential test.
A post hoc molecule bootstrap conditional on the fitted predictions gives a
$95\%$ interval for LO minus GM on nonane $\Delta H_{\mathrm{vap}}$ of
$[-0.006, +0.113]$ at budget $200$ (Supplementary Section~S4), and this
interval includes $0$. Within-fold baselines are in Supplementary Table~S7.

The protocol fixed
the box of half-width $2$, which binds the parent, and in the wide box
the gain shrinks to about zero (median $\Delta Q^2 = -0.002$ under the hybrid
control). We report the verdict as obtained. Its seed-count criterion
counts optimiser variance on fixed data, which we regard as a design
error, and after this result we made the measured boiling points of
Section~\ref{sec:bp} the primary predictive evidence.

Ridge regression on the degree-pair counts has higher point estimates than every index model on the nonanes, although its conditional interval against the Loyola model on $\Delta H_{\mathrm{vap}}$ includes zero. This comparison and those with the untuned point $LO(0,0,1)$ and the parameter-free $IRLA$ are in Supplementary Section~S11.

\subsection{Measured boiling points}\label{sec:bp}

Measured vaporisation enthalpies are too scarce for a larger test. Among
the acyclic alkanes C4--C16, NIST lists only $25$ skeletons outside $C_8$
and $C_9$, among them one of the $75$ decanes, with at least one calorimetric standard-state
$298$\,K determination. Two tests therefore used measured boiling
points from the NIST WebBook~\cite{NISTWebBook}, extracted by a rule
committed to the repository (commit \texttt{a5cf378}) before any model
was fitted. The rule admits all hexanes, heptanes and octanes, $34$ of
the $35$ nonanes and $34$ of the $75$ decanes. The included and
excluded decanes do not differ detectably in branching or imbalance
(Supplementary Section~S9), but with $34$ and $41$ molecules this does
not show that the decane cohort is representative.

Test~A refits and
reselects inside each leave-one-out fold on the decanes. Test~B pools
all $100$ molecules with the carbon number $n_C$ as a covariate. The
octane and nonane boiling points were used above, so Test~B is a pooled
size-adjusted analysis rather than a fresh external test. Both use the
wide-box design of Section~\ref{sec:ablation}, with
$(\alpha,\beta) \in (-12,12)^2$ for the parent and the same box with
$\gamma \in (-2,2)$ for the Loyola model, $2000$ candidates and seeds
$0$--$49$. The pre-registered criterion for a $\gamma$ effect required
at least $40$ wins in $50$ seeds and a molecule-bootstrap interval
excluding zero (Table~\ref{tab:bp}), which we read as requiring the lower end of the two-sided $95\%$ interval to exceed zero.

Both pre-registered verdicts are negative. On the decane cohort the
median $\Delta Q^2 = -0.072$, LO is ahead in $15$ of $50$ seeds, and the
$95\%$ interval is $[-0.156, +0.001]$. In the pooled size-adjusted set

On the decanes the point estimate therefore favours the parent, and the
Loyola model is behind in most seeds, but the interval includes zero, so
the test establishes neither a gain nor a loss from $\gamma$.

In the
original box of half-width $2$, reported as the pre-registration
requires, the two tuned models are level on the decanes (medians $0.469$
and $0.465$), and the Loyola model is marginally ahead on the pooled set
($0.980$ against $0.976$). Seed-by-seed comparisons across the two boxes were not registered, carry no interval and do not indicate a gain from $\gamma$. The pooled cohort was corrected after an external audit, and the uncorrected cohort gives the same verdict. Both are reported in Supplementary Section~S11.

An exploratory resampling reruns the whole nested
procedure for both models on random subsamples of $80\%$ of the
molecules. Each replicate uses a single candidate stream, so the ranges
also contain optimiser variance. On the decanes ($500$ subsamples) the median $\Delta Q^2$ is
$-0.110$ with central $95\%$ range $[-0.510, +0.312]$. On the pooled set
($200$ subsamples) it is $+0.001$ with range $[-0.015, +0.008]$.
Neither range supports a gain from $\gamma$. The two summaries differ in
training size and seed averaging and are not directly comparable. Nor are the ranges confidence intervals for the full-sample $\Delta Q^2$.

The selection-aware decane
range is so wide that it neither supports nor excludes gains of
practical size, the registered interval omits refitting and selection,
and no smallest effect of interest or equivalence margin was
registered. The registered contrast also gives LO$_h$ three dimensions
to search against two for GM with the same candidate budget, which
may handicap the three-parameter model (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). The
negative verdicts therefore mean that no gain was demonstrated under
these protocols; they do not establish equivalence, and they do not
show that $\gamma$ never helps.

Ridge
regression on the ten degree-pair counts reaches $Q^2 = 0.860$ on the
decanes, where every single index stays between $0.149$ and $0.514$
(medians over seeds for the tuned models), and
$0.991$ on the pooled set. On the registered secondary question of
Test~B, $IRLA$ and $LO(0,0,1)$ do not beat size plus the best classical
index ($0.963$ against $0.967$), while both tuned models have higher point estimates ($0.977$; no interval was registered for this secondary question).

A permutation check on the decanes repeats
the whole nested procedure, including the choice of the ridge penalty,
for $200$ random permutations of the boiling points. The permuted $Q^2$
has median $-0.09$, $95$th percentile $-0.01$ and maximum $0.20$. None reaches $0.860$, so the empirical $p$-value is $1/201 \approx 0.005$.
Wiener's two-descriptor model~\cite{Wiener1947}, with the path number $W$ and the polarity number, is compared with ridge in Supplementary Section~S11.

Under the stated boxes and candidate budgets, ridge regression predicts the boiling points more accurately than every single-index
model evaluated, with or without $\gamma$, in point estimate. The margin is large on the decanes and small on the pooled set, where size alone gives $Q^2 = 0.938$, and no interval for these differences is reported here. Part of this lead
reflects capacity: ridge fits one coefficient per count, the centred count
matrix has rank $7$ on the decanes and rank $8$ on the pooled set
(Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}), and each index model fits one descriptor
(with $n_C$ in Test~B). These conclusions concern acyclic alkanes.

\section{Concluding remarks}\label{sec:limitations}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

That the pure-imbalance points at nonzero algebraic $\gamma$ discriminate the
histogram of edge imbalances and nothing finer is Rada's
theorem based on the Lindemann--Weierstrass theorem~\cite[Theorem~3.4]{Rada2019ExpVDB}, a limit
that binds every index depending on an edge only through its degree
ratio~\cite[Proposition~2.5]{Rada2019ExpVDB}. The contribution here is
to locate where this limit binds. For trees the histogram carries the
full degree-pair information up to order twelve and loses it at order
thirteen, and for chemical trees at sixteen, with explicit colliding
families at every larger order. The minimality of these orders rests on
exhaustive enumeration. Along the imbalance
axis two chemical graphs exchange order at most five times. Off the ratio
plane $\gamma$ only reweights the same counts, which can restore
distinctions that the parent loses at particular rational exponents
(Section~\ref{sec:degeneracy}) and never destroys one at algebraic
$\gamma \neq 0$ and rational exponents.

Under random search at matched budgets, no predictive gain from the
imbalance parameter was demonstrated. A gain on vaporisation enthalpies met the seed-count criterion of
a pre-registered protocol, with a bootstrap interval that included zero.
It shrank to about zero in an exploratory comparison once the parent was
searched over a box that did not truncate it. Pre-registered tests on
measured boiling points were negative. On the decanes the point estimate
favoured the parent, with an interval that included zero and a
selection-aware range too wide to support or exclude gains of practical
size. There a linear model on the degree-pair counts had higher point
estimates than every single index.

The results of Sections~\ref{sec:geometry}--\ref{sec:bounds} are exact.
Their limits, and those of Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}, are of four
kinds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{Enumeration scope.} The minimality of the collision orders in
Theorem~\ref{thm:ratioplane}(iii), equality in the identifiability
bound of Supplementary Section~S10 for $\Delta \le 6$, and the
recovery of discrimination off $\Pi$ in Section~\ref{sec:degeneracy}
rest on exhaustive enumeration. No general argument is given.
\item[(ii)] \emph{Theoretical scope.} Whether two graphs with $\Delta \le 4$ can exchange order
five times along $\gamma$ is open (Section~\ref{sec:ratioplane}).
\item[(iii)] \emph{Data quality.} The data sets are small and acyclic: $18$ octanes, $35$
nonanes, $34$ decanes and $100$ pooled C6--C10 alkanes. Seven octane
entropies and the compiled acentric factors rest on secondary sources,
and the vaporisation enthalpy of 2,2,3,3-tetramethylbutane has an
unresolved reference state (Supplementary Section~S8).
\item[(iv)] \emph{Inferential scope.} The negative verdicts mean that no
gain was demonstrated. They do not show that $\gamma$ never helps, and
no equivalence margin was registered (Section~\ref{sec:bp}). Changes made after outcomes had been seen are listed in
Supplementary Table~S8. None changes a pre-registered verdict as
reported, but the wide-box analysis, in which the nonane gain shrinks
to about zero, is itself not pre-registered.
\end{enumerate}

The problem closest to these results is extremal: to determine the
extremal graphs for general parameter triples, beginning with the
maximal chemical trees of the pure-imbalance points, which
Section~\ref{sec:extremal} leaves open.
